# Crafting Europe

## Euro banknote design concept

### Independent alternative thematic study assessed under the published design-contest framework

### Theme

Crafting Europe

### Narrative

Natural materials acquire public value through knowledge, labour, exchange, maintenance, and common institutions.

### Denomination motifs

| Denomination | Material     | Civilisational function    |
| ------------ | ------------ | -------------------------- |
| €5           | Coal         | Energy                     |
| €10          | "Redstone"   | Automation and information |

| €50          | Copper       | Infrastructure             |

## 1. Executive summary

Crafting Europe presents six euro banknotes through the materials and systems of Minecraft.

Minecraft is a construction game set in a world made from cubic blocks. Players find natural resources, process them into usable materials, combine components, build structures, and connect mechanisms into larger systems. A block can be a mineral deposit, a unit of construction, a machine component, or part of a shared landscape. This makes the game suitable for representing the passage from matter to organised public life.

No prior knowledge of Minecraft is required to understand the banknotes. The principal images remain direct: coal fuels furnaces, a signal controls machinery, lapis supports books and learning, copper connects a city, emerald circulates through a market, and gold supports reserves and long-term systems. Familiarity with the game adds technical detail without controlling the primary reading.

Each denomination follows the same two-part narrative.

On the recto, the material appears in its natural setting. Geological conditions, altitude, vegetation, water, light, and scarcity explain where it comes from. The landscape remains dominant and is not yet organised around extraction.

On the verso, the material enters a social and technical system. Minecraft mechanics represent its real or analogous uses in energy, automation, culture, infrastructure, exchange, medicine, transport, and reserves.

The recurring proposition is:

> Natural resources acquire public meaning through knowledge, labour, exchange, maintenance, and responsible use.

The sequence is not a ranking of market value or rarity. It is an argument about the structure of civilisation.

Energy permits production.
Signals permit coordination.
Knowledge records, interprets, and redirects technical capacity.
Infrastructure connects people and institutions.
Exchange permits specialisation.
Reserves permit action across time.

Lapis lazuli is the deliberate cultural hinge. Without it, coal, "redstone," copper, and gold could produce a narrowly technical account of progress. The €20 inserts books, pigment, education, notation, mapping, and public memory between automation and infrastructure.

The series operates at two distances.

At first sight, denomination, dimensions, colour family, principal material, large numeral, and tonal silhouette provide recognition.

At close range, engraved landscapes, working mechanisms, material histories, architectural scenes, diagrams, borders, maps, and links between denominations provide sustained attention.

The governing requirement is:

> Legible in one second, but not exhausted in one second.

## 2. Theme, narrative, and motifs

### 2.1 Theme

Crafting Europe treats Europe as a constructed environment rather than a fixed collection of monuments.

Its subject is the accumulated work through which resources become tools, knowledge, buildings, routes, markets, institutions, and reserves. Construction includes maintenance, repair, adaptation, and reuse. The theme therefore concerns continuity as much as invention.

### 2.2 Narrative

The series follows one narrative across six denominations:

> Matter becomes common life through organised knowledge and cooperation.

The recto establishes origin and potential. The verso establishes transformation and consequence.

The narrative is cumulative rather than linear. Later systems continue to depend on earlier ones. Information requires energy. Infrastructure requires knowledge. Markets require roads, standards, and records. Reserves require productive systems that give stored value a purpose.

### 2.3 Motifs

The six ores function as denomination motifs rather than as isolated subjects.

Coal represents usable heat and the beginning of industrial capacity.

"Redstone" represents switching, timing, sensing, logic, and automated control.

Lapis lazuli represents pigment, books, education, notation, and knowledge applied to tools and public work.

Copper represents conductive networks, water systems, roofs, public lighting, observation, repair, and visible ageing.

Emerald represents exchange, recognised value, professional specialisation, and confidence between participants.

Gold represents reserve capacity, standards, transport, timekeeping, medicine, scientific use, and continuity.

### 2.4 Why ores

An ore contains both material fact and unrealised possibility.

Its appearance on the recto explains that value does not begin as an abstract claim. It begins in a place, under physical conditions, with limits and consequences.

Its transformation on the verso explains that no material carries a complete civic meaning by itself. Public value is created through methods, labour, standards, exchange, and institutions.

## 3. Assessment framework

The published design-contest framework requires a coherent narrative across the €5, €10, €20, €50, €100, and €200 denominations, clear discrimination between notes, accessibility, technical feasibility, common European elements, and consistency between theme and imagery. It also requires provision for the European flag, the map of Europe, a signature area, tactile identification, and later integration of security features. The technical brief retained the current denomination-specific euro formats.[1]

The jury assessed eight visual and technical aspects:

| Criterion                                           | Published weighting |
| --------------------------------------------------- | ------------------: |
| Visual harmony and coherence of the complete series |                 10% |
| Thematic consistency with the story                 |                 10% |
| Visual appeal and aesthetic quality                 |                 20% |
| Symbolism and cultural representation of the EU     |                 10% |
| Discrimination between denominations                |                 10% |
| Public recognition                                  |                  5% |
| Accessibility                                       |                 10% |
| Creativity and originality                          |                 25% |

These aspects accounted for 60% of the overall assessment. Required design and technical items accounted for the remaining 40%.[1]

Crafting Europe is organised around the same requirements.

One visual grammar governs all six notes. Each denomination then changes material, landscape, colour family, border motif, constructed environment, and civic subject.

European representation is carried through shared achievements and systems rather than national emblems: industry, electrical research, computation, education, books, art, public infrastructure, standards, trade, law, navigation, medicine, and long-term institutional capacity.

Security devices are not presented as finished technical features. The composition reserves suitable locations while remaining complete without simulated foil, holographic, or optically variable effects.

## 4. Conceptual basis

### 4.1 Minecraft as a design language

Minecraft is used as a system of representation, not as an applied entertainment image.

Its relevance comes from four characteristics.

First, the world is materially explicit. Stone, wood, metals, minerals, water, soil, plants, and manufactured objects remain visibly distinct.

Second, transformation is understandable. A resource is found, processed, combined, transported, stored, maintained, and used. The relationship between source and result remains visible.

Third, complex systems emerge from a limited vocabulary of components. Blocks can produce farms, railways, logic machines, factories, libraries, cities, and public works.

Fourth, construction is collective and cumulative. Buildings and systems can be inherited, repaired, extended, adapted, or connected to earlier work.

This allows European history to be represented through practices and institutions rather than only portraits or landmarks.

### 4.2 Comprehension without prior game knowledge

Every primary scene has an ordinary visual reading.

A minecart carries fuel.
A distributed signal controls machines.
Books, pigments, and maps preserve knowledge.
Roofs, lamps, bridges, and water channels form infrastructure.
A public square organises exchange.
A reserve supports transport, treatment, and planning.

Minecraft knowledge reveals why specific components are present and how they function. It is an additional level of reading, not an entry requirement.

### 4.3 European scope

Minecraft's modular world does not prescribe a nationality. A bridge, library, foundry, school, market, or observatory can belong to several European traditions at once.

This permits the banknotes to combine shared forms without assigning one country the role of representing the whole continent.

The map of Europe appears without internal borders. It is treated as a field of coastlines, rivers, routes, settlements, and institutional connections.

### 4.4 Civilisation as the principal subject

No denomination is organised around a heroic protagonist.

Small players or villagers may demonstrate work, exchange, study, or maintenance, but no figure dominates the composition. The central subject is the system that outlives any one participant.

This differs from a portrait series without rejecting the importance of individual achievement. It directs attention toward the environments, methods, and institutions through which achievements become shared.

### 4.5 Visual fidelity to Minecraft

The world must be recognised as Minecraft rather than as a generic fantasy setting assembled from cubes.

Recognition comes from canonical terrain behaviour, familiar block textures, stepped silhouettes, game-scale vegetation, characteristic object proportions, and recognisable mechanisms. It does not come from drawing a dark line around every block.

Adjacent blocks of the same material should often read as one surface. Block boundaries emerge through texture changes, shadow, stepped edges, and changes of material. Continuous contour lines around every cube are avoided because they turn natural terrain into masonry and make the image announce its cubical construction too insistently.

Recto landscapes remain landscapes. They do not become geological cutaways, mantle sections, exploded diagrams, or views through the earth. Ore appears where a normal exterior rock face, cave opening, ravine, or exposed formation makes it visible.

The illustrations are neither unmodified screenshots nor medieval voxel-fantasy scenes. They preserve Minecraft's visual vocabulary while using banknote composition, selective print linework, controlled colour, and formal hierarchy.

Buildings may exceed the scale or practicality of ordinary play. They need not be literal survival-mode constructions. They must, however, remain native to Minecraft in materials, proportions, construction language, and recognisable objects. Smooth fantasy masonry, invented block types, ornamental carving unrelated to the game, and generic voxel architecture are excluded.

The governing art direction is:

> Preserve the world; elevate the representation.

## 5. Reference to Design E

Design E provides a methodological reference rather than a composition to reproduce.

Its published explanation placed emphasis on the value of each person's work rather than celebrity. A circle and a field of symbols created an intellectual space that was familiar at first sight and richer under examination. The reverse connected the principal figure with cultural achievements and consequences. The jury noted its classical organisation, clear typography, approachable palette, alignment between both sides, and continuity with the current euro series.[1][2]

Crafting Europe adapts four principles from that approach.

First, one denomination motif provides immediate recognition.

Second, a stable recurring device gives the series a common visual anchor.

Third, the reverse expresses the consequences of the recto subject in public life.

Fourth, monetary hierarchy is preserved through alignment, borders, typography, tonal control, and areas of rest.

The adaptation is intentionally less centralised.

The natural landscape remains the dominant recto image. The recurring ore label is offset to the right and behaves as an identification medallion rather than a central portrait field. It contains only the canonical ore block. It does not explain the material's later uses, which belong exclusively to the verso.

The reverse does not repeat the ore as an emblem or provide a diagram of its transformation. It presents a constructed world whose organisation makes the material's role visible.

The relationship can be stated as:

> Landscape: where the material belongs.
> Ore label: what the material is.
> Verso: the world shaped through its use.

## 6. Narrative structure

### 6.1 Recto: origin

The recto presents the material before possession.

The ore appears naturally within a recognisable Minecraft environment. Its location communicates altitude, geology, scarcity, access, and environmental context.

Human figures are absent or extremely limited. No scene is composed as a triumphant extraction image.

The recto answers three questions:

1. Where is the material found?
2. What makes that environment distinctive?
3. How visible, scarce, or accessible is the deposit?

The recto remains a coherent landscape. It does not reveal underground strata that could not be seen from the chosen viewpoint.

### 6.2 Ore label: identification

The offset ore label isolates one faithful isometric in-game ore block from the landscape.

It functions as the denomination identifier. Its stable position, pale field, and consistent cube scale give the series a repeatable point of recognition.

The label contains no ore name, usage symbols, arrows, diagrams, or secondary objects. The block is sufficient.

### 6.3 Verso: constructed world

The verso presents the material after it has entered a network of knowledge and labour.

The reverse is a civic landscape, not a recipe. It does not divide the material into simplified stages, use arrowed pictograms, or force the scene into one continuous chain.

Uses are understood from the relationships between workshops, institutions, transport, architecture, tools, agriculture, learning, exchange, and public life. The viewer reads the material's role from the world itself.

Each reverse answers three questions:

1. How does the material participate in the constructed environment?
2. Which public functions depend on it?
3. What larger capacity does that environment support?

### 6.4 Series progression

The sequence is structured as an argument.

Coal provides usable energy.

"Redstone" converts energy into timed, sensed, and repeatable action.

Lapis records knowledge, transmits methods, and joins technical capacity to culture.

Copper gives those methods durable physical networks.

Emerald organises exchange between specialised participants.

Gold stores capacity so that systems can act beyond the immediate present.

Lapis is the point at which the series rejects a purely mechanical account of development. It establishes that books, images, notation, education, and memory are infrastructure in their own right.

## 7. Monetary design principles

### 7.1 Visual authority

A banknote must possess material and institutional authority before its theme is examined.

Minecraft is therefore integrated into the graphic language of currency through:

controlled borders;

layered typography;

tonal overprinting;

fine line systems;

maps;

number fields;

serial areas;

seals and institutional marks;

reserved tactile and security zones.

Minecraft's own textures and silhouettes remain visible. Monetary structure organises the world; it does not repaint every surface as historical engraving.

### 7.2 Detail as evidence of value

Traditional banknote complexity performs a visual as well as technical role. Dense, ordered information indicates care, control, productive difficulty, and institutional capacity.

The notes should appear to contain more information than can be absorbed immediately.

Fine detail is concentrated in ore textures, geological transitions, machinery, roofs, masonry, bookshelves, public spaces, borders, maps, and material surfaces.

This density is not uniform. Areas around the principal denomination, signature field, European flag, and selected portions of sky, stone, water, parchment, or cave darkness provide visual rest.

### 7.3 Complexity without clutter

Every dense field has a clear hierarchy.

Large shapes establish the note first. Medium-scale scenes explain the story. Fine detail rewards inspection.

No detail competes with the denomination numeral or makes the institutional information difficult to locate.

### 7.4 Two distances of reading

From a distance, the note is identified through:

denomination;

dimensions;

dominant hue;

principal ore;

large-scale silhouette;

distribution of dark and light fields.

At close range, it reveals:

recognisable Minecraft mechanisms;

real material histories;

small workshops;

transport and civic networks;

links between denominations;

ore-specific patterns;

European maps and public symbols;

cycles of extraction, use, maintenance, and reuse.

### 7.5 Printed Minecraft rather than engraved voxel fantasy

The target is a hybrid print language.

Canonical or near-canonical Minecraft textures carry the world. Fine banknote linework is added selectively to shadows, borders, transitions, maps, clouds, and secondary ornament. It should not trace every cube or replace the game's texture language with universal cross-hatching.

Paper tone, restrained overprint, guilloche, stipple, and limited engraving provide monetary character. Crisp block surfaces, familiar textures, and game-scale proportions provide Minecraft identity.

Glossy volumetric rendering, cinematic bloom, exaggerated fog, smooth fantasy stone, and uniformly dense intaglio treatment are excluded.

### 7.6 Horizontal use

All primary elements remain horizontal.

The notes are designed for counting, stacking, fanning, passing, and storage in wallets. Numerals and lettering do not require rotation.

Contemporary character is achieved through subject, structure, and technique rather than by transferring the habits of a phone interface.

## 8. Colour policy

### 8.1 General rule

All denominations use mildly saturated colour.

The ore determines the hue family. Paper, engraving, neutral mineral tones, and layered overprint control its intensity.

> Colour appears printed into the note, not emitted by it.

The palette should resemble ink absorbed into a physical substrate. It should not reproduce the full brightness of Minecraft on a backlit display.

High-chroma accents are limited to approximately 3 to 5 percent of the visible surface. They identify ore fragments, powered signal lines, lapis pigment, copper highlights, emerald facets, and selected gold contacts.

### 8.2 Shared palette structure

Each denomination uses four colour levels:

a near-white paper or lens tone;

a mildly saturated dominant hue;

a dark structural tone;

a small high-chroma accent.

Tonal movement is produced through line density, stippling, cross-hatching, and translucent overprint rather than smooth digital gradients.

### 8.3 Denomination palette

| Denomination | Dominant family       | Supporting tones                        |
| ------------ | --------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| €5           | Graphite grey         | limestone, charcoal, muted alpine green |
| €10          | Oxidised red          | burgundy, deepslate, pale rose-grey     |
| €20          | Weathered ultramarine | slate blue, parchment, cool grey        |
| €50          | Aged copper           | umber, warm paper, limited verdigris    |
| €100         | Mineral green         | forest green, pale mint-grey, stone     |
| €200         | Antique gold          | ochre, cream, terracotta, dark brown    |

### 8.4 Lapis saturation

Lapis lazuli remains recognisably blue without making the €20 a deep blue digital field.

The darkest ultramarine is confined to the ore block, pigment, and selected written details. Most of the note is carried by weathered blue, slate, parchment, and cool grey.

### 8.5 Grayscale performance

The series must retain denomination hierarchy when colour is removed.

Different border rhythms, tonal masses, ore silhouettes, tactile groups, and scene structures provide recognition independent of hue.

## 9. Series-wide composition and common visual architecture

### 9.1 Format and proportion

The six denominations retain the horizontal formats and denomination-specific proportions of the current euro series, as required by the technical brief.[1]

The common system is a proportional grid rather than one identical rectangle.

Approximate working ratios are:

| Denomination | Approximate ratio |
| ------------ | ----------------: |
| €5           |            1.94:1 |
| €10          |            1.90:1 |
| €20          |            1.85:1 |
| €50          |            1.82:1 |
| €100         |            1.91:1 |
| €200         |            1.99:1 |

The final image area ends at the note edge. Presentation margins, skew, perspective distortion, drop shadows, and photographed-paper surroundings are not part of the design.

### 9.2 Shared recto composition

The recto contains three principal fields.

The left value field contains the dominant numeral, a smaller repeated value, tactile reservation, and a low-density background that preserves immediate recognition.

The central field contains the uninterrupted source landscape. It is read as one exterior or cave-space view, not as a collage of landscape and geological section.

The right institutional field contains the European flag, EURO, ΕΥΡΩ, and ЕВРО, the signature placeholder, the common Europe map, secondary denomination information, and reserved technical areas.

The ore label is offset toward the right and overlaps the transition between landscape and institutional field. It remains secondary to the landscape while providing a fixed denomination identifier.

### 9.3 Shared verso composition

The verso contains a value field, a broad constructed landscape, and an institutional field.

The central panorama is the conceptual centre of the reverse. Workshops, institutions, transport, streets, agriculture, machines, interiors, and civic spaces form one integrated environment.

There is no arrowed stage strip, no lower recipe panel, no row of usage pictograms, and no mandatory rail or circuit line crossing the note. Rails, roads, wiring, water channels, and routes may appear where the depicted world genuinely requires them, but they do not operate as explanatory overlays.

The uses of the material must read from spatial organisation and visible activity.

### 9.4 Shared alignments

All denominations retain stable proportional alignments for:

the upper edge of the principal numeral;

the centre of the ore label;

the baseline of the three euro scripts;

the flag field;

the Europe map field;

the signature and serial reservations;

the inner frame margin.

These alignments provide coherence while allowing different landscapes and civic scenes.

### 9.5 Density and quiet zones

Highest detail density appears in:

the natural ore exposure;

the principal architecture or machinery;

areas of concentrated work or public activity;

the common map;

the middle and outer border systems.

Lower-density zones surround:

the main numeral;

the ore block inside its pale label;

the signature field;

the European flag and euro scripts;

selected portions of sky, water, cave darkness, stone, or paper tone.

### 9.6 What remains fixed and what changes

| Remains fixed                           | Changes by denomination            |
| --------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| Horizontal orientation                  | Physical proportion                |
| Recto and verso composition             | Natural landscape                  |
| Ore-label position and relative scale   | Canonical ore block                |
| Numeral hierarchy                       | Dominant hue                       |
| Institutional field                     | Constructed landscape              |
| Europe map geometry and placement logic | Material uses visible in the scene |
| Flag treatment                          | Border geometry                    |
| Typography system                       | Tonal distribution                 |
| Tactile reservation                     | Architectural emphasis             |
| Density logic                           | Environmental conditions           |

### 9.7 Borders

Each note carries two principal border systems:

an outer legal and denomination frame;

a middle pattern derived from the material's geometry or environment.

The borders do not contain carousels of uses or miniature recipes. Their role is structural, rhythmic, and denomination-specific.

| Denomination | Border logic                                                  |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| €5           | Geological strata and coal speck rhythm                       |
| €10          | Branching signal geometry                                     |
| €20          | Pigment grain, manuscript ruling, and restrained glyph rhythm |
| €50          | Conductive bands and oxidation fields                         |
| €100         | Exchange lattice and market paving geometry                   |
| €200         | Concentric reserve rings and measured gold facets             |

### 9.8 Numerals and lettering

The denomination numeral and principal scripts use a pixel-derived type system recognisably connected to Minecraft's canonical lettering.

It must not default to a generic sans-serif face. Latin, Greek, and Cyrillic forms are designed as one family, preserving the square rhythm and compact spacing associated with the game while remaining legible as monetary typography.

Small technical text may use a restrained monospaced companion face.

No game logo appears. All final lettering is controlled artwork and is not generated as part of scene imagery.

### 9.9 Institutional integration

The European flag, euro scripts, signature field, and map must appear embedded in the note rather than attached as flat digital rectangles.

They share the paper texture, tonal printing, and denomination palette of the surrounding composition while remaining clearly identifiable.

The Europe map uses one stable silhouette, projection, orientation, and proportion across the whole series. Denomination changes affect colour and internal texture, not geography.

### 9.10 The twelve stars

The official twelve-star symbol remains accurate and clearly recognisable.

Secondary star structures may appear in stained glass, lamps, paving, or beacon architecture, but they do not alter the official flag.

## 10. The ore label

### 10.1 Function

Every recto contains one ore label positioned consistently toward the right side.

It overlaps the source landscape and the institutional field but does not occupy the geometric centre. The landscape remains the principal image. The label functions as a denomination identifier and repeated series emblem.

It has four roles:

1. identify the material immediately;
2. isolate the canonical ore block from landscape perspective and lighting;
3. provide a stable visual anchor across all six notes;
4. reserve a coherent area for later watermark, foil, or window development.

It does not explain uses. That argument belongs to the reverse.

### 10.2 Shape and scale

The standard form is circular or very slightly stepped at its outer edge.

Its overall diameter, position, and internal cube scale remain consistent relative to note height throughout the series.

The label must not resemble a magnifying glass, alchemical seal, orbit diagram, or interface widget.

### 10.3 Tonal field

The field is white or near-white, derived from the denomination hue at very low saturation and high lightness.

A very fine denomination-specific texture prevents the area from appearing pasted onto the note:

| Denomination | Label texture                       |
| ------------ | ----------------------------------- |
| €5           | Sedimentary hatching                |
| €10          | Faint signal traces                 |
| €20          | Manuscript ruling and pigment grain |
| €50          | Conductive mesh                     |
| €100         | Exchange lattice                    |
| €200         | Concentric reserve rings            |

The texture remains subordinate to the ore block.

### 10.4 Central object

One faithful isometric Minecraft ore block is centred inside the label.

Every denomination uses the same viewing angle, apparent cube dimensions, lighting direction, distance from the frame, and visual scale.

The block remains canonical in texture and construction. It is not regenerated as a new mineral sculpture for each note. Coal ore must look like coal ore rather than a black-topped terrain block; redstone, lapis, copper, emerald, and gold follow the same rule.

The label contains no ore name, explanatory text, usage symbol, arrow, secondary block, material-state sequence, or orbiting icon.

### 10.5 Edge construction

The edge contains a fine inner rule, a restrained denomination-specific line pattern, and a slightly heavier outer rule.

There is no drop shadow, glass reflection, transparent magnification effect, digital glow, or ring of pictograms.

The label must appear printed into the note.

### 10.6 Relationship to both sides

The recto landscape states where the material belongs.

The label states what the material is.

The verso shows the constructed world in which the material acquires public meaning.

This three-part reading is the central compositional logic of the series.

## 11. Architectural language and human presence

### 11.1 Architectural language

The built environments remain unmistakably native to Minecraft.

They use recognisable materials, block proportions, roof logic, openings, stairs, slabs, rails, workstations, vegetation, and mechanical objects. Their scale and density may exceed ordinary gameplay, and some scenes may combine spaces more compactly than a player build would.

Literal buildability is not the test. Visual nativeness is.

The architecture may draw from broad European periods and civic types:

Romanesque massing;

Gothic vertical structure;

Renaissance proportion and courtyards;

industrial halls, bridges, and stations;

modern libraries, laboratories, housing, and public utilities.

These references are translated through Minecraft's own vocabulary. They must not drift into a generic medieval voxel world with invented carved stone, rounded fantasy roofs, ornamental assets, or custom materials unrelated to the game.

No single monument is reproduced as the emblem of Europe.

### 11.2 Terrain and perspective

Recto terrain follows recognisable Minecraft morphology: stepped cliffs, continuous material surfaces, familiar vegetation scale, plausible cave openings, and ore exposure consistent with the visible viewpoint.

The camera presents one coherent scene. It does not combine perspective landscape with cutaway geology.

Verso panoramas may be idealised, but their spatial logic remains clear. Buildings, machines, streets, rails, channels, and interiors belong to one environment rather than separate infographic panels.

### 11.3 Human and villager figures

Figures remain small in relation to systems and buildings.

They demonstrate work, study, exchange, repair, or civic use.

No figure looks directly outward as a central protagonist. No denomination is organised around a character portrait.

Villagers appear only where their game mechanics are conceptually necessary, especially on the emerald note.

### 11.4 Cultural plurality

Building details, crops, roof forms, paving, workshops, and public spaces may vary across the series.

These differences create a plural European environment without assigning a fixed national identity to any denomination.

## 12. Accessibility and denomination discrimination

Colour is supported by independent identification systems.

Each denomination differs through:

dimensions;

large numeral;

ore silhouette;

overall tonal balance;

border pattern;

tactile mark group;

scene composition.

The tactile system increases progressively across the series. Its exact physical form requires specialist testing, but a consistent zone is reserved near both short edges.

| Denomination | Visual identification pattern      |
| ------------ | ---------------------------------- |
| €5           | Broad horizontal strata            |
| €10          | Branching signal lines             |
| €20          | Repeated written and pigment forms |
| €50          | Alternating conductive bands       |
| €100         | Interlocking market lattice        |
| €200         | Concentric rings                   |

The main numeral remains legible under partial occlusion, low contrast, and reduced viewing size.

No denomination relies on small differences between neighbouring hues.

The near-white ore label gives every material one high-contrast identification point, but the lens is supplementary. Denomination recognition must remain possible when it is partly covered.

## 13. Environmental continuity and stewardship

The series does not equate extraction with progress.

The recto establishes that each material first belongs to a landscape. The verso shows that its public value depends on how it is processed, directed, maintained, and reused.

A recurring lifecycle appears across the six denominations:

1. origin;
2. extraction or intake;
3. processing;
4. public use;
5. maintenance;
6. repair or reuse.

The lifecycle is expressed differently on each note.

Coal includes exhausted ground, limited smoke, replanting, and a transition away from indiscriminate fuel use.

"Redstone" emphasises efficiency, sensing, timing, and reduction of repetitive labour.

Lapis connects material extraction with preservation, teaching, restoration, and transmission.

Copper shows weathering, maintenance, replacement, and reuse within long-lived public systems.

Emerald presents value as circulation through a community rather than accumulation by one actor.

Gold contrasts closed storage with reserves directed toward transport, treatment, research, and continuity.

This does not turn the notes into environmental campaigns. It establishes responsibility as part of material history.

## 14. Reading the banknote

The design supports a consistent sequence of attention.

### 14.1 First glance

The viewer recognises denomination, dimensions, colour family, numeral, and material.

### 14.2 Source

The recto landscape explains where the ore appears and how it relates to its environment.

### 14.3 Identification

The near-white ore label isolates the canonical block in a stable location.

### 14.4 Constructed world

On the verso, the viewer reads a complete landscape of work, institutions, routes, architecture, and public life.

### 14.5 Civic meaning

The relationships inside that landscape reveal the denomination's principal function: energy, automation, knowledge, infrastructure, exchange, or continuity.

### 14.6 Close examination

Borders, material states, architectural details, maps, and links to other denominations provide further interpretation.

This sequence permits immediate use while preserving visual reserves for repeated handling.

## 15. €5: Coal

### 15.1 Civilisational function

Energy

### 15.2 Rationale

Coal begins the series because heat precedes the later processes shown on the other denominations.

It is common within Minecraft and appears early in play. Its availability does not diminish its importance. It provides fuel for cooking, lighting, smelting, and early production.

The historical association is equally direct. Coal supported steam power, furnaces, rail transport, iron and steel production, urban heating, and large-scale industry. It helped reorganise production and distance by making concentrated heat available at an unprecedented scale.

That history also carries environmental and social consequences. The note does not present smoke as a neutral emblem of progress. Exhausted ground, atmospheric residue, labour, restoration, and the reduced place of coal in a later energy system remain visible within the narrative.

The €5 denomination suits a material that is frequently used, widely recognised, and foundational.

### 15.3 Recto

A recognisable Minecraft mountain landscape occupies the central panorama.

A broad exposed stone cliff rises above alpine grass blocks, small flowers, sparse snow, and a distant valley. Surfaces use canonical or near-canonical stone, dirt, grass, snow, and coal-ore textures. Adjacent stone blocks read as continuous terrain rather than individually outlined masonry.

A natural coal seam appears on the visible cliff face. It is not presented through a cutaway or a view through the mountain.

The offset ore label uses a near-white graphite tint with fine sedimentary hatching. One canonical isometric coal-ore block is centred inside it at the common series scale.

The value field uses broad horizontal strata.

The atmosphere is cool and quiet. No torch, tunnel entrance, mining machine, use symbol, or material diagram appears.

### 15.4 Verso

The reverse depicts a Minecraft industrial landscape organised around coal, heat, transport, and metallurgy.

Minecarts bring coal from a mountain tunnel into a district containing canonical chests, hoppers, furnaces, blast furnaces, rails, storage buildings, anvils, and smithing areas. Iron ore enters the industrial system elsewhere in the scene, and finished metal supports workshops, bridges, stations, and public buildings.

The information is carried by the panorama itself. There is no arrowed sequence, recipe panel, pictogram strip, or obligatory rail running across the whole note as an explanatory device.

Smoke remains limited and is integrated into the sky and print texture rather than used as a dramatic effect.

A restored edge of the quarry contains replanted vegetation and reused material, placing industrial history beside maintenance and recovery.

### 15.5 Close-reading elements

Close inspection reveals hopper connections, furnace inputs and outputs, minecart loading, rail switches, an anvil workshop, a smithing area, iron bridge construction, storage practice, and repaired ground.

The far bridge recalls European iron engineering without reproducing one monument.

Border detail uses coal speck rhythm and geological strata rather than miniature usage icons.

### 15.6 Colour

Graphite grey dominates.

Limestone and pale paper create contrast. Alpine green remains muted. Warm orange appears only inside active furnaces.

## 16. €10: "Redstone"

### 16.1 Civilisational function

Automation and information

### 16.2 Definition

"Redstone" is a fictional Minecraft material.

Within the game, it carries signals and controls mechanisms. Redstone dust, torches, repeaters, comparators, observers, pistons, lamps, and related components allow players to build switches, clocks, automatic doors, farms, transport controls, storage systems, calculators, and other logical machines.

Its conceptual value lies in the clarity of cause and effect. A visible signal travels through a visible system and produces a repeatable action. That makes it an effective analogy for electrical networks, relays, sensing, logic, computation, and automated control without claiming that one real substance performs all of those roles.

### 16.3 Rationale

Coal represents energy. "Redstone" represents direction.

Its real-world reference is not one substance. It is a composite analogy for electrical circuits, switching, timing, communication, sensing, logic, and computation.

The €10 denomination introduces the transition from industrial power to controlled systems.

The note makes the analogy understandable without requiring knowledge of individual game components. A viewer sees that a signal travels through an organised network and produces repeatable actions.

### 16.4 Recto

A recognisable Minecraft deepslate cavern fills the central scene.

The ceiling is layered and low. Water, dripstone, and distant lava establish depth without turning the cavern into fantasy concept art.

A redstone ore vein appears naturally in the visible rock. Canonical deepslate and redstone textures remain legible. Blocks are not separated by continuous contour lines.

The offset ore label uses a near-white red-grey tint with extremely fine signal traces. One canonical isometric redstone-ore block is centred inside it at the common series scale.

The value field contains branching red geometry.

No constructed mine, use symbol, circuit diagram, or material sequence appears on the recto.

### 16.5 Verso

The reverse depicts a civic and productive environment organised by distributed redstone systems.

Recognisable redstone dust, torches, repeaters, comparators, observers, pistons, lamps, note blocks, hoppers, and related components appear within working spaces. The scene includes an automated farm, item sorting, controlled doors, rail signalling, lighting, a compact logic hall, and a research or teaching space.

These systems coexist inside one constructed environment. They are not arranged as a single circuit diagram, linear recipe, or sequence of arrowed applications.

The viewer understands automation through repeated cause and effect across the scene: crops are harvested, goods are sorted, doors respond, lamps indicate state, and transport is controlled.

### 16.6 European historical layer

The note refers to European traditions in electricity, mathematics, communication, computing, and control systems through the organisation of the built world rather than through a didactic technology timeline.

Switching, timing, sensing, comparison, memory, and calculation are visible in working Minecraft mechanisms. Historical meaning emerges from their combined role in the civic environment.

### 16.7 Close-reading elements

A small logic module resembles a binary adder, another stores state, and a lamp array forms twelve stars.

Signal routes connect agricultural, industrial, transport, musical, and research functions without becoming an ornamental overlay.

Border detail uses branching signal geometry rather than component pictograms.

### 16.8 Colour

The red is deep, oxidised, and mildly saturated.

Dark burgundy and deepslate provide structure. Pale rose-grey paper fields preserve readability.

Bright red is restricted to powered lines and signal nodes.

## 17. €20: Lapis lazuli

### 17.1 Civilisational function

Knowledge and culture

### 17.2 Rationale

Lapis lazuli introduces a deliberate change in the series.

Coal and "redstone" describe energy and control. Copper later describes infrastructure. Without an intervening cultural denomination, the series would imply that civilisation progresses only through increasingly advanced machinery.

Lapis establishes knowledge, language, education, art, and memory as equally structural.

In Minecraft, lapis lazuli is used in enchanting. Enchanting improves tools, weapons, armour, and books through a system associated with bookshelves, written symbols, accumulated experience, and specialised knowledge.

For a viewer unfamiliar with the game, the scene reads as a library, school, laboratory, and artists' workshop. For a player, the enchanting system provides a direct mechanical relationship between study and improved capability.

The real material reinforces the theme. Lapis lazuli has been traded over long distances, ground into blue pigment, used in manuscript illumination and painting, and valued for the labour required to select, prepare, and apply it. The material therefore connects geology with craft, images, records, and cultural transmission.

The €20 denomination is appropriate because it is practical and frequently handled. Knowledge and culture are presented as ordinary public resources, not remote luxuries. The note becomes a working denomination about the work of learning.

### 17.3 Colour decision

Minecraft lapis is often represented by a deep blue.

The banknote does not reproduce that colour at full digital intensity.

The dominant field uses a weathered ultramarine moderated by:

slate blue;

cool grey;

parchment;

pale mineral blue.

The darkest blue is confined to ore fragments, pigment deposits, written details, and a limited number of focal points.

The result remains recognisably blue while avoiding the appearance of a screen graphic or toy package.

### 17.4 Recto

A broad Minecraft stone cavern contains a natural lapis deposit.

The space is quieter and more open than the €10 cavern. Water descends along one wall and gathers in a shallow pool. Pale stone and cool grey rock keep the scene from becoming a saturated blue field.

The ore seam appears on a visible cavern surface. Canonical lapis-ore texture provides the darkest blue accents.

The offset ore label uses a near-white blue-grey tint with manuscript ruling and microscopic pigment grain. One canonical isometric lapis-ore block is centred inside it at the common series scale.

The value field uses repeated horizontal lines related to manuscript ruling and geological layering.

No book, pigment bowl, enchanting symbol, or usage diagram appears on the recto.

### 17.5 Verso

The reverse is organised as a shared house of knowledge.

A pigment and manuscript workshop, library, learning hall, enchanting space, classroom, map room, observatory, stained-glass workshop, bookbinding area, laboratory table, and public reading space coexist within one Minecraft civic complex.

Lapis is ground and used as pigment. Books are written, stored, studied, and applied. Enchanting is treated as the game's visual equivalent of accumulated knowledge improving practical capability.

Improved tools appear in surveying, construction, agriculture, conservation, and repair elsewhere in the landscape.

The scene contains no arrowed chain from stone to book, no strip of applications, and no abstract line that tells the viewer how to read it. Knowledge is visible through the relationships among rooms, people, objects, and public uses.

### 17.6 European historical layer

The note represents culture as an active system.

Books are written, copied, translated, stored, taught, interpreted, and used.

Pigment connects natural material to art.

Maps connect observation to navigation.

Libraries connect individual study to public memory.

Schools connect one generation to the next.

Workshops connect knowledge to practice.

The architecture draws from European libraries, cloisters, university halls, scriptoria, civic schools, and studios without reproducing one institution.

### 17.7 Close-reading elements

A stained-glass arrangement contains twelve stars. Books, maps, pigment preparation, copying, binding, observation, teaching, and restoration appear at several scales.

Abstract book marks remain non-linguistic. The common Europe map is repeated only in the institutional field and is not redrawn differently inside the scene.

Border detail uses pigment grain and manuscript rhythm rather than a miniature production sequence.

### 17.8 Colour

Weathered ultramarine is the dominant hue.

Parchment and cool grey provide most of the large fields. Blue-black is used for the deepest engraving. Small muted gold details may appear in manuscript decoration, but they remain secondary to the €200 palette.

## 18. €50: Copper

### 18.1 Civilisational function

Infrastructure

### 18.2 Copper or iron

Iron carries major historical and technical significance. In Minecraft it is used for tools, armour, rails, hoppers, minecarts, buckets, pistons, anvils, and many structural systems.

Its visual identity is less suitable for the €50 denomination. Minecraft iron ore is pale and earthy, creating insufficient separation from the grey €5 and yellow €200.

Copper provides a clearer orange-brown denomination family and a wider visual narrative.

Its material history connects:

metalworking;

alloys;

vessels;

roofing;

water systems;

electrical conduction;

communication;

motors;

public architecture;

repair and reuse.

In the real world, copper links ancient metalworking to modern electrical systems. It appears in alloys, vessels, roofs, water systems, motors, telecommunications, power distribution, and many renewable-energy technologies. Weathered copper surfaces can develop a green patina, while green copper minerals such as malachite provide a further material connection between copper and green oxidation products.

Minecraft copper also changes visibly as it ages. Bright orange surfaces become green through oxidation. That transformation allows the note to represent infrastructure across time.

Copper is therefore selected as the principal material. Iron remains visible as a supporting component in rails, tools, hoppers, and structural frames.

### 18.3 Recto

A recognisable Minecraft dripstone cave or weathered stone chamber contains a broad copper-ore vein.

Granite, pointed dripstone, shallow water, raw copper formations, and warm grey rock establish the setting. Orange mineral points dominate, with restrained green staining near moisture as an early indication of weathering.

The ore appears on visible surfaces rather than inside a geological cutaway.

The offset ore label uses a near-white warm-copper tint with a microscopic conductive mesh. One canonical isometric copper-ore block is centred inside it at the common series scale.

The value field uses alternating bands related to conductivity and architectural seams.

No roof, lightning rod, spyglass, lamp, or material-state diagram appears on the recto.

### 18.4 Verso

The reverse depicts a Minecraft city whose public systems depend on copper.

Smelting and preparation workshops sit within a broader environment of copper roofs, public lighting, lightning protection, bridges, water channels, observatory structures, transport buildings, and maintenance yards.

A major civic roof changes gradually from orange to green, allowing time and weathering to appear within the architecture. A lightning rod protects a building. A spyglass supports observation, and a brush is used in an archaeological workspace.

The city is read as an interconnected environment rather than a list of applications. There is no conductor overlay crossing the note, no stage panel, and no arrowed material sequence.

### 18.5 European historical layer

The note connects early metalworking, vessels, roofs, water infrastructure, electrical distribution, communication, public transport, observation, maintenance, and reuse.

These meanings are distributed through the city. The principal subject is the network inside and around architecture rather than one monumental building.

Iron remains visible throughout as rails, fastenings, hoppers, tools, and structural frames. It is the recurring structural material of the series rather than the principal subject of one denomination.

### 18.6 Close-reading elements

Four visible copper states show progressive oxidation across roofs and maintained surfaces.

Water channels, lamps, lightning protection, workshops, observation, archaeology, and material reuse appear as parts of daily civic life.

Border detail uses conductive bands and oxidation fields rather than a row of use symbols.

### 18.7 Colour

Aged copper orange dominates.

Umber and warm paper provide structure. Verdigris is restricted to weathered surfaces, water reflections, and selected secondary lines.

The saturation remains moderate across both sides.

## 19. €100: Emerald

### 19.1 Civilisational function

Exchange and trust

### 19.2 Emerald or copper

A green copper denomination could be justified through oxidation and public architecture.

That choice would weaken two aspects of the series.

First, it would separate the material from its most immediate colour identity.

Second, it would remove emerald, which has a direct monetary role in Minecraft.

Emeralds function as the medium of exchange used in trading with villagers. Villagers have specialised professions and offer different goods or services. The system provides a clear visual model of trade, mutual dependence, valuation, and repeated exchange.

Emerald is therefore selected.

### 19.3 Rationale

Emerald has less structural or industrial importance than coal, copper, or iron.

That difference is productive.

Its value depends on rarity, appearance, recognised quality, skilled preparation, provenance, and confidence in exchange. Physical usefulness does not explain its price by itself.

Currency also depends on collective recognition, verification, institutional continuity, and confidence that others will accept it.

The €100 note therefore examines value as a social relationship rather than a property contained entirely inside the object.

The question is not only what an emerald can physically do. The question is why people accept it in exchange for other forms of labour and material.

### 19.4 Recto

A high Minecraft mountain landscape contains a single naturally exposed emerald-ore block.

Snow, sparse grass, cloud layers, pale stone, and a steep valley establish altitude and difficulty of access. Scarcity is expressed through isolation rather than ornament.

The exposed ore belongs to the visible cliff surface. The view does not reveal the mountain interior.

The offset ore label uses a near-white mineral-green tint with an extremely fine exchange lattice. One canonical isometric emerald-ore block is centred inside it at the common series scale.

The value field contains interlocking lines meeting at small nodes.

No bell, ledger, balance, trade symbol, or exchange diagram appears on the recto.

### 19.5 Verso

A broad public square forms the centre of a prosperous Minecraft town.

Villagers of several professions occupy connected farms, workshops, stalls, libraries, map rooms, quarries, roads, bridges, wells, and meeting spaces. Emeralds circulate through visible exchanges, but no interface boxes or schematic trade cards appear.

The scene focuses on mutual dependence. Farmers, librarians, cartographers, masons, toolsmiths, armourers, shepherds, clerics, and fletchers each contribute to a town that none could sustain alone.

The market is read through spatial relations and activity rather than a road drawn as an explanatory line or a strip of barter stages.

### 19.6 Real-material layer

A small lapidary and verification workspace shows rough emerald being examined, cut, recorded, and set.

Judgement, craft, provenance, verification, and recognised standards connect the physical stone to wider systems of trust. These actions remain part of the market environment rather than a separate grading diagram.

### 19.7 European historical layer

The note represents:

markets;

guilds;

accounting;

trade routes;

banking;

civic law;

public squares;

specialised labour;

shared standards.

No profession is presented as self-sufficient.

The farmer depends on tools.
The toolsmith depends on food and materials.
The librarian depends on buildings and paper.
The builder depends on maps, measurements, and exchange.

The emerald circulates because the town already functions as a network.

### 19.8 Close-reading elements

Twelve paving stones form a star arrangement. A map stall, repair workshop, public ledger space, and maintained infrastructure reinforce the relation between exchange and civic continuity.

Trade is visible through objects and actions rather than interface graphics or symbolic pairings.

Border detail uses market paving and exchange lattice geometry rather than a timeline from barter to electronic payment.

### 19.9 Colour

The green is mineral and restrained.

Dark forest green provides depth. Pale mint-grey and warm stone prevent the field from becoming uniformly green.

Bright emerald appears only in ore fragments, exchanged objects, and selected network nodes.

## 20. €200: Gold

### 20.1 Civilisational function

Reserve and continuity

### 20.2 Rationale

Gold concludes the series because it combines physical use, exchange value, ceremonial meaning, and long-term storage.

Its practical roles include reliable conduction, corrosion-resistant contacts, reflective surfaces, precision instruments, specialised medical and scientific applications, and thin coatings used where dependable performance matters more than bulk quantity.

Within Minecraft, gold has several distinct functions:

powered rails;

clocks;

golden food associated with healing;

beacons;

decorative construction;

barter with piglins.

Gold tools also have limited durability. The material is therefore not presented as universally superior.

The €200 note concerns the responsible direction of stored value.

A reserve is meaningful when it supports continuity, transport, treatment, research, and action beyond the immediate moment.

### 20.3 Recto

A Minecraft badlands landscape fills the central field.

Layered terracotta mesas rise above a ravine. Gold ore appears naturally on a visible stone surface within the landscape. Sparse vegetation and eroded pillars establish age and dryness.

A distant abandoned mineshaft may intersect a hillside, but the recto remains an exterior landscape rather than a cutaway through the earth.

The offset ore label uses a near-white warm-cream tint with concentric reserve rings. One canonical isometric gold-ore block is centred inside it at the common series scale.

The value field uses measured concentric geometry.

No clock, rail, beacon, apple, balance, or use symbol appears on the recto.

### 20.4 Verso

The reverse contains the broadest civic panorama in the series.

An assay and reserve district sits within a larger environment of transport, timekeeping, scientific observation, treatment, public space, and exchange. A controlled vault holds gold blocks without becoming a treasure chamber. Powered rails serve real transport needs inside the landscape. A clock hall and observatory contain maps and instruments. A treatment garden contains brewing stands and golden apples. A beacon rises above a civic square.

A Nether portal connects to a balanced piglin exchange area, showing that recognised value can support exchange between distinct systems.

These uses are integrated into one world. There is no row of icons, no arrowed recipe, and no powered rail imposed as an explanatory band across the note.

### 20.5 European historical layer

The note brings together standards and assay, art and conservation, navigation and timekeeping, medicine, scientific instruments, reserves, transport, and exchange.

The contrast between closed storage and resources directed toward public systems is communicated through the relation between the vault and the surrounding city, not through a didactic border sequence.

The visual argument is precise:

> Stored value acquires public legitimacy through responsible use.

### 20.6 Close-reading elements

Clocks show several times, indicating coordination across distance. The beacon base contains twelve gold points. Scientific equipment uses small gold surfaces where dependable contact or reflection is required.

The piglin exchange area is orderly and balanced. Neither side is presented as subordinate.

Border detail uses reserve rings and measured facets rather than miniature use symbols.

### 20.7 Colour

The note uses antique gold, ochre, cream, terracotta, and dark brown.

Bright metallic yellow is restricted to small contacts, rail elements, ore fragments, and the beacon structure.

Brilliance is created through line contrast rather than glossy effects.

## 22. Continuity across denominations

Each note contains one controlled reference to the next stage in the series.

The coal works contain an early signal mechanism.

The "redstone" research hall contains books and notation.

The lapis learning hall contains diagrams for civic construction and copper systems.

The copper city contains an emerald market.

The emerald market contains a controlled gold reserve.

The gold assay room begins with a coal-fired furnace.

The sequence therefore forms a cycle.

Later systems do not erase earlier materials. They organise, maintain, and reinterpret them.

## 23. Security-ready visual opportunities

The imagery provides suitable areas for later technical development without depending on those features for its primary composition.

Potential transformations include:

an ore block or fragment as watermark;

the ore label as foil, window, or optically variable structure;

material weathering or mineral points as colour-changing elements;

ore-specific tactile patterns;

microtext within borders and institutional fields;

denomination-specific changes within small accent regions.

The ore label is especially suitable because it has fixed position, stable scale, high local contrast, and one clearly recognisable object.

Any technical transformation must preserve:

the large numeral;

the euro scripts;

the European flag;

the common Europe map;

the landscape narrative;

the legibility of the canonical ore block.

Functional security development requires a separate specialist phase.

## 24. Recognition and public comprehension

The primary narrative does not require familiarity with Minecraft.

The first reading remains direct:

coal fuels a furnace;

a circuit controls machines;

books and pigments preserve and apply knowledge;

copper supports a city;

a market depends on exchange;

gold supports reserves and long-term systems.

Game knowledge adds a second reading:

hoppers and blast furnaces;

repeaters and comparators;

enchanting and experience;

copper oxidation and lightning rods;

villager professions;

powered rails, beacons, and piglin barter.

The near-white ore label assists viewers unfamiliar with the landscapes by isolating one repeated object in a stable location.

The series therefore combines public recognition with specialist detail.

## 25. Excluded elements

The banknotes must not resemble:

a game screenshot;

a generic medieval voxel world;

a children's product;

a promotional poster;

a digital interface;

a flat illustration placed on a coloured rectangle;

an existing euro note with block imagery added afterward;

a fantasy coupon;

a plausible reproduction of current legal tender.

The following elements do not appear:

heads-up display;

inventory bar;

crosshair;

floating labels;

game logo;

oversized player character;

unrelated creatures;

comic facial expressions;

photorealistic human portraits;

large neon effects;

fully saturated colour fields;

continuous dark outlines around every cube;

geological cutaways combined with perspective landscapes;

ore names inside the label;

usage pictograms on the recto;

orbiting icons around the ore block;

arrowed recipe strips on the verso;

glass-like ore labels;

drop shadows behind the ore block;

generated pseudo-lettering;

outer presentation margins, skew, or perspective distortion in final design plates.

The unusual subject requires no comic emphasis. Execution remains formal, disciplined, and institutionally credible.

## 26. Jury presentation

The final submission should contain:

a complete series overview;

six recto designs;

six verso designs;

paired recto-verso plates;

enlarged ore-label details;

enlarged landscape and civic-scene details;

a colour and grayscale plate;

an accessibility plate;

a common-composition diagram;

a fixed-versus-variable system diagram;

a narrative-continuity diagram;

a concise technical note on reserved security areas.

Each denomination should first be presented without annotation so that it can be assessed as a banknote.

Explanatory diagrams should follow the principal images.

Production procedures, layer inventories, generation prompts, and assembly instructions belong to separate design documents and are not part of the jury-facing proposal.

## 27. Evaluation summary

### 27.1 Visual harmony and coherence

All six notes share one horizontal structure, one recto-verso narrative, one offset ore-label system, one typography system, one hybrid print language, and one restrained palette policy.

### 27.2 Thematic consistency

Every denomination begins with a natural material and ends with a constructed world shaped by its use.

Minecraft mechanics are selected because they explain each material rather than because they are recognisable references.

### 27.3 Visual appeal and aesthetic quality

Ordered density, canonical Minecraft textures, selective banknote linework, architecture, landscape, pale label fields, and restrained colour provide depth without sacrificing denomination recognition.

### 27.4 Symbolism and cultural representation of the EU

Europe is represented through shared achievements and systems:

industry;

engineering;

education;

books and art;

public infrastructure;

trade and standards;

law and accounting;

scientific inquiry;

navigation;

medicine;

long-term institutional capacity.

The absence of a single protagonist supports a plural account of European achievement.

### 27.5 Denomination discrimination

Each note has a distinct physical proportion, colour family, canonical ore block, landscape, border pattern, and tonal distribution.

### 27.6 Public recognition

The large numeral, stable ore-label position, direct material narrative, and recognisable Minecraft world remain immediate.

Familiarity with the game is not required.

### 27.7 Accessibility

Dimensions, numerals, contrast, tactile reservations, border rhythms, ore silhouettes, and scene structures support identification beyond colour alone.

### 27.8 Creativity and originality

Minecraft is treated as a formal grammar of matter, transformation, and collective construction.

The theme is integrated into the structure of currency rather than applied as an image layer.

## 28. Closing statement

Crafting Europe presents value as a process.

The recto begins with a material whose future use is not yet determined.

The offset ore label identifies that material through one canonical in-game block.

The verso shows the systems through which it becomes socially useful.

Coal becomes energy.
"Redstone" becomes coordination.
Lapis becomes knowledge and culture.
Copper becomes infrastructure.
Emerald becomes exchange.
Gold becomes continuity.

The sequence describes Europe as a shared environment made through resources, learning, specialisation, standards, maintenance, and cooperation.

A banknote performs a related transformation at a smaller scale.

Printed matter becomes a bearer of value because a public system gives it form, recognition, and trust.
