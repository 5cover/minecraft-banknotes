# Crafting Europe

## Euro banknote design concept

### Alternative thematic study assessed under the published design-contest framework

### Series

€5: Coal
€10: "Redstone"
€20: Lapis lazuli
€50: Copper
€100: Emerald
€200: Gold

## 1. Executive summary

Crafting Europe presents six euro banknotes through the materials and systems of Minecraft.

Minecraft is a construction game set in a world composed of cubic blocks. Players gather natural resources, process materials, combine components, build structures, and create mechanical systems. Its visual language is simple, but its underlying subject is broad: matter becomes tools, infrastructure, knowledge, exchange, and shared environments.

That structure supports a coherent banknote narrative.

On the recto, each denomination shows an ore in its natural setting. The material remains embedded in a landscape, before extraction or transformation. Geological conditions, altitude, vegetation, water, light, and scarcity establish its origin.

On the verso, the material enters a social and technical system. Minecraft mechanics show what can be made from it: fuel, automation, education, architecture, transport, trade, medicine, or reserves.

The recurring narrative is:

> Natural resources acquire public meaning through knowledge, labour, exchange, and responsible use.

The six denominations form a progression of civilisational functions:

| Denomination | Material     | Principal meaning          |
| ------------ | ------------ | -------------------------- |
| €5           | Coal         | Energy                     |
| €10          | "Redstone"   | Automation and information |
| €20          | Lapis lazuli | Knowledge and culture      |
| €50          | Copper       | Infrastructure             |
| €100         | Emerald      | Exchange and trust         |
| €200         | Gold         | Reserve and continuity     |

The sequence is not a ranking of market value or geological rarity. It is a structured account of how societies develop.

Energy permits production.
Signals permit coordination.
Knowledge gives technical activity meaning and continuity.
Infrastructure connects people and institutions.
Exchange permits specialisation.
Reserves permit long-term action.

The series is intended to operate at two distances.

At first sight, each denomination is identified through its dimensions, number, dominant hue, ore silhouette, border rhythm, and general composition.

At close range, engraved landscapes, working mechanisms, architectural scenes, material diagrams, repeated symbols, micro-patterns, and visual continuities between notes provide further information.

The banknotes remain immediately legible without becoming visually finite.

## 2. Assessment framework

The design-contest framework requires a coherent narrative across the six denominations, clear discrimination between notes, accessibility, technical feasibility, common European elements, and consistency between theme and imagery. It also requires provision for the European flag, the map of Europe, a signature area, tactile identification, and later integration of security features.

The jury assessed eight visual and technical aspects:

| Criterion                                           | Published weighting |
| --------------------------------------------------- | ------------------: |
| Visual harmony and coherence of the complete series |                 10% |
| Thematic consistency with the story                 |                 10% |
| Visual appeal and aesthetic quality                 |                 20% |
| Symbolism and cultural representation of the EU     |                 10% |
| Discrimination between denominations                |                 10% |
| Public recognition                                  |                  5% |
| Accessibility                                       |                 10% |
| Creativity and originality                          |                 25% |

These aspects accounted for 60% of the overall assessment. Required design and technical items accounted for the remaining 40%.

Crafting Europe is organised around the same requirements.

The theme is expressed through a single visual grammar across all denominations. Each note has a distinct material, colour family, landscape, mechanical logic, and civilisational subject. European representation is carried through shared achievements, public institutions, scientific methods, material culture, trade, education, architecture, and collective construction rather than through national monuments.

The design remains horizontal, consistent with the physical use of a banknote and with the structure of the current euro series.

Security devices are not designed as finished technical features at this stage. Areas are reserved for later development, while the composition remains complete without relying on holographic or optically variable effects.

## 3. Conceptual basis

### 3.1 Minecraft as a design language

Minecraft is used as a system of representation, not as decoration.

Its relevance comes from four characteristics.

First, the world is materially explicit. Stone, wood, metals, minerals, water, soil, plants, and manufactured objects remain visually distinct.

Second, transformation is understandable. A resource is found, processed, combined, transported, stored, and used. The relationship between source and result remains visible.

Third, complex systems can emerge from simple components. A small vocabulary of blocks can produce farms, railways, calculating machines, factories, libraries, cities, and public works.

Fourth, construction is collective and cumulative. Buildings and systems can be inherited, repaired, extended, adapted, or connected to the work of others.

These characteristics allow Minecraft to represent European history without reducing it to a sequence of portraits or isolated landmarks.

Europe is presented as a constructed environment shaped by materials, knowledge, standards, exchange, repair, and cooperation.

### 3.2 Relevance to different generations

The visual language is familiar to many younger Europeans, but recognition does not depend on prior experience with the game.

A viewer unfamiliar with Minecraft is still able to identify:

a mountain containing coal;

a circuit controlling machinery;

a library and workshop devoted to knowledge;

a city supported by copper infrastructure;

a market organised around exchange;

a reserve used to support transport, medicine, and long-term planning.

Knowledge of the game adds another interpretive layer. It is not required for the primary narrative.

### 3.3 European scope

The notes avoid national symbols and literal rankings of individual countries.

European identity is expressed through shared fields of achievement:

energy and industrialisation;

electrical engineering and computation;

books, universities, pigments, art, and scientific learning;

metalworking, architecture, public utilities, and communication;

markets, civic law, accounting, and specialised labour;

reserves, navigation, medicine, transport, and continuity.

Architectural references combine forms found across Europe without reproducing one specific national monument as the dominant image.

The map of Europe appears without internal borders. It functions as a field of connected landscapes, routes, waters, settlements, and institutions.

## 4. Narrative structure

### 4.1 Recto: origin

The recto presents the material before possession.

The ore appears naturally within a recognisable Minecraft environment. Its location provides information about altitude, geology, scarcity, and access.

Human figures are absent or extremely limited. The landscape is not yet organised around extraction.

The principal question is:

> Where does value begin?

### 4.2 Verso: transformation

The verso presents the material after it enters a network of knowledge and labour.

The material is not shown as a finished trophy. It participates in a system.

Each reverse moves horizontally through three stages:

source or intake;

processing or organisation;

public result.

The principal question is:

> What becomes possible when a material enters a shared system?

### 4.3 Series progression

The sequence contains one deliberate interruption.

Coal, "redstone," and copper can be read as a technical and industrial group. Lapis lazuli prevents the series from treating development as a purely mechanical process.

The €20 introduces culture, education, language, art, and accumulated knowledge between automation and infrastructure.

This gives the sequence a broader account of civilisation:

> Power without knowledge remains limited.
> Knowledge without infrastructure remains isolated.
> Infrastructure without exchange remains incomplete.
> Exchange without continuity remains fragile.

## 5. Monetary design principles

### 5.1 Visual authority

A banknote must possess material and institutional authority before its theme is examined.

Minecraft is therefore rendered through the graphic language of currency:

fine engraving;

cross-hatching;

geometric line systems;

repeated borders;

layered typography;

tonal overprinting;

small diagrams;

maps;

number fields;

serial areas;

seals and institutional marks;

areas reserved for tactile and security elements.

Block geometry provides the underlying construction. It does not replace monetary composition.

### 5.2 Detail as information

The notes' complexity embody care, control, and productive difficulty.

The viewer perceives more information than can be absorbed immediately. This density remains ordered.

Fine detail is concentrated in:

ore textures;

geological transitions;

machine sequences;

roofing and masonry;

transport systems;

bookshelves and diagrams;

borders;

material-state illustrations;

the map field.

Areas around the principal denomination, signature field, and main ore silhouette remain quieter.

### 5.3 Two distances of reading

From a distance, the note is identified through:

denomination;

dimensions;

dominant hue;

principal ore;

large-scale silhouette;

distribution of dark and light fields.

At close range, it reveals:

working Minecraft mechanisms;

material histories;

small workshops;

transport routes;

hidden links between denominations;

ore-specific patterns;

European maps and civic symbols;

cycles of extraction, use, maintenance, and reuse.

### 5.4 Horizontal use

All primary elements remain horizontal.

The notes are designed for counting, stacking, fanning, passing, and storage in wallets. Numerals and lettering do not require rotation.

Contemporary character is achieved through subject, structure, and technique rather than through imitation of a phone screen.

## 6. Colour policy

### 6.1 General rule

All denominations use mildly saturated colour.

The ore determines the hue family. Paper, engraving, overprint, and neutral mineral tones control its intensity.

> The colour appears printed into the note, not emitted by it.

Large fields of luminous or fully saturated colour would make the series resemble digital entertainment graphics. Restrained colour gives the imagery physical weight and allows fine linework to remain visible.

The brightest form of each hue is confined to small focal details:

ore fragments;

powered "redstone" lines;

lapis pigment;

copper highlights;

emerald facets;

gold contact points.

### 6.2 Shared palette structure

Each denomination uses four colour levels:

a pale paper tone;

a mildly saturated dominant hue;

a dark structural tone;

a small high-chroma accent.

Smooth digital gradients are avoided. Tonal movement is produced through line density, stippling, layered printing, and transitions between engraved textures.

### 6.3 Denomination palette

| Denomination | Dominant family       | Supporting tones                        |
| ------------ | --------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| €5           | Graphite grey         | limestone, charcoal, muted alpine green |
| €10          | Oxidised red          | burgundy, deepslate, pale rose-grey     |
| €20          | Weathered ultramarine | slate blue, parchment, cool grey        |
| €50          | Aged copper           | umber, warm paper, limited verdigris    |
| €100         | Mineral green         | forest green, pale mint-grey, stone     |
| €200         | Antique gold          | ochre, cream, terracotta, dark brown    |

The entire series retains clear hierarchy and denomination recognition in grayscale.

# 7. Series-wide composition and common visual architecture

Each denomination follows a common scaffold.

## 6.1 Recto layout

The left 18 percent forms the value tower.

It contains the primary denomination numeral, a smaller repeated numeral, tactile identification marks, and a denomination-specific linear pattern.

The central 52 percent contains the source landscape. It is panoramic, atmospheric, and built from recognisable Minecraft terrain.

An ore lens overlaps the landscape between the central and right fields. It may be circular, elliptical, or constructed from concentric block-stepped rings. Inside it, the ore block is shown more clearly, with geological lines and material symbols around it.

The right 30 percent contains the institutional field: the European flag, the three scripts for the word euro, the signature placeholder, the map of Europe, issue text, and secondary denomination.

The map should be rendered as a block-grid relief without internal national borders. Rivers, mountain chains, and coastlines may be suggested through engraved contour lines.

## 6.2 Verso layout

The left 15 percent repeats the denomination anchor.

The central 70 percent contains the civilisational panorama. This is the visual and conceptual centre of each note.

The right 15 percent contains the map field, serial-number zone, twelve-star structure, and a compact material diagram.

A continuous process line runs across the reverse. Depending on the denomination, it may be a railway, redstone circuit, measurement grid, copper conductor, trade road, or powered rail.

This line unifies the scene and makes its sequence readable.

## 6.3 Numerals

The denomination numerals should be large, calm, and unmistakable.

At a distance, the numeral must dominate every decorative detail.

## 6.4 Lettering

The principal scripts should remain formal and institutional.

EURO, ΕΥΡΩ, and ЕВРО appear in a consistent horizontal hierarchy, in the Minecraft typeface. Small technical text may use a companion monospaced face, suggesting measurement, coordinates, and engineering notation.

## 6.5 The twelve stars

The twelve stars remain accurate and clearly recognisable.

Secondary star motifs may be translated into block-built constellations, experience-orb paths, or mechanical nodes, but the official flag itself should not be distorted.

## 6.6 Borders

Each note carries three border layers:

an outer legal and denomination frame;

a middle guilloche derived from the ore’s geometry;

an inner narrative frame containing miniature symbols of the material’s uses.

The borders should look printed rather than rendered. Line breaks, overprinting, slight tonal variation, and restrained imperfections should create physical presence.

The geometry varies by denomination:

| Denomination | Border logic                                                       |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| €5           | Geological strata                                                  |
| €10          | Branching circuits                                                 |
| €20          | Pigment particles, written lines, and enchantment glyph structures |
| €50          | Conductive bands and oxidation stages                              |
| €100         | Exchange lattice and market routes                                 |
| €200         | Concentric reserve rings and powered-rail rhythm                   |

### 6.7 Typography

The denomination numeral uses a the "Minecraft" pixelated typeface with classical proportions and slightly stepped internal construction.

Small technical text may use a restrained monospaced companion face to suggest coordinates, measurement, and engineering notation.

## 8. Accessibility and denomination discrimination

Colour is supported by independent identification systems.

Each denomination differs through:

dimensions;

large numeral;

ore silhouette;

overall tonal balance;

border pattern;

tactile mark group;

scene composition.

The tactile system increases progressively across the series. Its exact physical form requires specialist testing, but a consistent zone is reserved near the short edges.

| Denomination | Visual identification pattern              |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------ |
| €5           | Broad horizontal strata                    |
| €10          | Branching signal lines                     |
| €20          | Repeated written and faceted pigment forms |
| €50          | Alternating conductive bands               |
| €100         | Interlocking market lattice                |
| €200         | Concentric rings                           |

The principal numeral remains legible under partial occlusion and low-contrast viewing.

No denomination relies on subtle differences between neighbouring hues.

## 9. €5: Coal

### 9.1 Civilisational function

Energy

### 9.2 Rationale

Coal begins the series because heat precedes the later processes shown on the other denominations.

It is common within Minecraft and appears early in play. Its availability does not diminish its importance. It provides fuel for cooking, lighting, smelting, and early production.

The historical association is equally direct. Coal supported furnaces, transport, metallurgy, and industrial production.

The design does not present coal as an uncomplicated symbol of progress. Its contribution to industrial development is shown together with smoke, exhausted ground, and the need for repair.

The €5 denomination suits a material that is frequently used, widely recognised, and foundational.

### 9.3 Recto

A mountain surface occupies the central panorama.

Grey stone cliffs rise above a narrow band of alpine grass. Small flowers, exposed soil, snow patches, and distant peaks establish altitude.

A coal seam appears where the rock face has fractured naturally. It remains part of the mountain.

The ore lens enlarges one block of coal ore. Concentric geological lines surround it. Four small symbols identify later applications:

flame;

furnace arch;

rail wheel;

iron ingot.

The value field uses broad horizontal strata.

The atmosphere is cool and quiet. No torch, tunnel entrance, or miner appears.

### 9.4 Verso

Minecarts carry coal from a tunnel into a compact industrial district.

The central system contains mechanically credible Minecraft components:

input chests;

hoppers;

furnaces;

blast furnaces;

output chests;

rails;

powered sections;

storage halls.

Iron ore enters through a separate channel. Ingots emerge from the furnace system and move toward workshops.

The industrial district opens into a town containing:

rail infrastructure;

stone and brick workshops;

an iron bridge;

a foundry hall;

storage depots;

public buildings supported by the transport network.

Smoke remains limited and is rendered as engraved tonal structure rather than a dominant cloud.

A small restoration area at the edge of the quarry contains replanted trees and reused building material.

The railway forms the continuous process line.

### 9.5 Close-reading elements

Furnace fronts generate the inner border pattern.

Rail sleepers structure the lower frame.

A small section diagram shows coal entering the furnace and metal leaving it.

An anvil, smithing table, rail workshop, and bridge yard appear as distinct working areas.

The far bridge recalls nineteenth-century iron engineering without reproducing a specific monument.

### 9.6 Colour

Graphite grey dominates.

Limestone and pale paper create contrast. Alpine green remains muted. Warm orange appears only inside active furnaces.

## 10. €10: "Redstone"

### 10.1 Civilisational function

Automation and information

### 10.2 Definition

"Redstone" is a fictional Minecraft material.

Within the game, it carries signals and controls mechanisms. Redstone dust, torches, repeaters, comparators, observers, pistons, lamps, and related components allow players to build switches, clocks, automatic doors, farms, transport controls, storage systems, calculators, and other logical machines.

### 10.3 Rationale

Coal represents energy. "Redstone" represents direction.

Its real-world reference is not one substance. It is a composite analogy for electrical circuits, switching, timing, communication, sensing, logic, and computation.

The €10 denomination introduces the transition from industrial power to controlled systems.

The note makes the analogy understandable without requiring knowledge of individual game components. A viewer sees that a signal travels through an organised network and produces repeatable actions.

### 10.4 Recto

A deep deepslate cavern fills the central scene.

The ceiling is layered and low. Water, dripstone, and distant lava establish depth without dominating the composition.

A redstone ore vein appears within the dark rock. Small red mineral points provide the principal colour accent.

The ore lens enlarges one block. Geological cracks gradually become a circuit diagram.

Small component symbols identify:

switching;

timing;

comparison;

movement;

light.

The value field contains branching red lines with circular terminals.

No constructed tunnel appears. The material remains unprocessed.

### 10.5 Verso

A continuous redstone circuit crosses the note.

It begins as a simple line and enters a sequence of working components:

redstone torches;

repeaters;

comparators;

observers;

pistons;

lamps.

The central field contains a modular logic system. Rows of components resemble an early computing room while remaining consistent with Minecraft construction.

The signal then divides into practical applications:

an automatic crop farm;

an item-sorting system;

a railway signal;

a piston bridge;

a public entrance;

a note-block music mechanism.

A university or research tower contains a larger calculating system and lamp display.

The circuit remains visually continuous from left to right.

### 10.6 European historical layer

The lower border presents a sequence of abstracted technologies:

mechanical switch;

telegraph pulse;

relay;

electronic valve;

transistor;

integrated circuit;

logic gate.

These references are translated into Minecraft forms rather than imported as photographic objects.

The note represents scientific and technical traditions in electricity, mathematics, communication, computing, and systems engineering.

### 10.7 Close-reading elements

A small redstone module resembles a binary adder.

Another contains memory cells.

A lamp array forms twelve stars.

The European map is crossed by signal routes connecting rural, urban, industrial, and research areas.

The system remains plausible to viewers familiar with Minecraft mechanics.

### 10.8 Colour

The red is deep, oxidised, and mildly saturated.

Dark burgundy and deepslate provide structure. Pale rose-grey paper fields preserve readability.

Bright red is restricted to powered lines and signal nodes.

## 11. €20: Lapis lazuli

### 11.1 Civilisational function

Knowledge and culture

### 11.2 Rationale

Lapis lazuli introduces a deliberate change in the series.

Coal and "redstone" describe energy and control. Copper later describes infrastructure. Without an intervening cultural denomination, the series would imply that civilisation progresses only through increasingly advanced machinery.

Lapis establishes knowledge, language, education, art, and memory as equally structural.

In Minecraft, lapis lazuli is used in enchanting. Enchanting improves tools, weapons, armour, and books through a system associated with bookshelves, written symbols, accumulated experience, and specialised knowledge.

For a viewer unfamiliar with the game, the scene reads as a library, school, laboratory, and artists' workshop. For a player, the enchanting system provides a direct mechanical relationship between study and improved capability.

The real material reinforces the theme through its association with blue pigment, decorative work, manuscripts, and skilled preparation.

The €20 denomination is appropriate because it is practical and frequently handled. Knowledge and culture are presented as everyday public resources, not remote luxuries.

### 11.3 Colour decision

Minecraft lapis is often represented by a deep blue.

The banknote does not reproduce that colour at full digital intensity.

The dominant field uses a weathered ultramarine moderated by:

slate blue;

cool grey;

parchment;

pale mineral blue.

The darkest blue is confined to ore fragments, pigment deposits, written details, and a limited number of focal points.

The result remains recognisably blue while avoiding the appearance of a screen graphic or toy package.

### 11.4 Recto

A broad stone cavern contains a natural lapis ore deposit.

The space is quieter and more open than the €10 cavern. Water descends along one wall and gathers in a shallow pool. Pale stone and cool grey rock prevent the blue from occupying the full field.

The ore seam is visible but not oversized. Blue fragments appear within the stone.

The ore lens combines two structures:

a mineral cross-section;

a circular arrangement of books, written lines, pigment particles, and geometric symbols.

Small application symbols identify:

book;

pigment bowl;

enchanting table;

map;

stained glass;

written page.

The value field contains repeated horizontal lines that resemble both manuscript ruling and geological layering.

### 11.5 Verso

The reverse is organised as a shared house of knowledge.

The left field contains a pigment and manuscript workshop.

Lapis is ground, prepared, and applied to illustrated pages. Tables hold brushes, books, glass vessels, and carefully arranged mineral fragments.

The central field contains a library and learning hall built through Minecraft architecture.

Bookshelves surround an enchanting table. The table is treated as a symbolic instrument of accumulated knowledge rather than a source of spectacle. Fine engraved symbols move from the bookshelves toward tools and written volumes.

Adjacent spaces contain:

a classroom;

a map room;

a small observatory;

a stained-glass workshop;

a copying and bookbinding area;

a laboratory table;

a public reading space.

The right field shows knowledge applied.

Improved tools are used in careful construction, surveying, agriculture, and restoration. The purpose is not to present magical objects. It is to show that study changes what a society can do.

A written line crosses the note and becomes, in succession:

a geological contour;

a manuscript rule;

a line of notation;

a map route;

an architectural measurement.

### 11.6 European historical layer

The note represents culture as an active system.

Books are written, copied, translated, stored, taught, interpreted, and used.

Pigment connects natural material to art.

Maps connect observation to navigation.

Libraries connect individual study to public memory.

Schools connect one generation to the next.

Workshops connect knowledge to practice.

The architecture draws from European libraries, cloisters, university halls, scriptoria, civic schools, and studios without reproducing one institution.

### 11.7 Close-reading elements

A twelve-star arrangement appears in a stained-glass window.

Bookshelf patterns form part of the inner border.

A tiny sequence shows rough lapis, ground pigment, painted page, stored book, and public reading.

Several books contain abstract marks, never generated pseudo-language.

A small map aligns visually with the Europe map in the institutional field.

The enchanting symbols are restrained and line-based. They resemble notation more than glowing fantasy effects.

### 11.8 Colour

Weathered ultramarine is the dominant hue.

Parchment and cool grey provide most of the large fields. Blue-black is used for the deepest engraving. Small muted gold details may appear in manuscript decoration, but they remain secondary to the €200 palette.

## 12. €50: Copper

### 12.1 Civilisational function

Infrastructure

### 12.2 Copper or iron

Iron carries major historical and technical significance. In Minecraft it is used for tools, armour, rails, hoppers, minecarts, buckets, pistons, anvils, and many structural systems.

Its visual identity is less suitable for the €50 denomination. Minecraft iron ore is pale and earthy, creating insufficient separation from the grey €5 and yellow €200.

Copper provides a clearer orange-brown denomination family and a wider visual narrative.

Its material history connects:

metalworking;

alloys;

vessels;

roofing;

water systems;

electrical conduction;

communication;

motors;

public architecture;

repair and reuse.

Minecraft copper also changes visibly as it ages. Bright orange surfaces become green through oxidation. That transformation allows the note to represent infrastructure across time.

Copper is therefore selected as the principal material. Iron remains visible as a supporting component in rails, tools, hoppers, and structural frames.

### 12.3 Recto

A dripstone cave or weathered stone chamber contains a broad copper ore vein.

Granite, raw copper formations, pointed dripstone, shallow water, and warm grey rock establish the setting.

Orange mineral points dominate. Limited green staining appears near water as an early indication of surface change.

The ore lens shows three material states:

ore;

raw copper;

refined ingot.

Small symbols identify:

roof tile;

lightning rod;

conductor;

spyglass;

brush;

lamp.

The value field contains alternating bands resembling conductive paths and architectural seams.

### 12.4 Verso

The reverse depicts a city supported by copper systems.

The left field contains smelting, cutting, and preparation workshops.

The central field contains:

copper roofs;

public lighting;

lightning rods;

bridges;

water channels;

observatory structures;

transport buildings;

maintenance workshops.

A large civic roof changes gradually from orange to green across the composition. The sequence makes time visible.

A lightning strike reaches a rod and is directed safely into the building system.

A player uses a spyglass from an observatory. Another uses a brush in a controlled excavation area. These two actions connect observation of the distant world with study of the buried past.

Copper lamps form a public lighting network.

Engraved conductor lines pass beneath streets and through public buildings. They represent electrical and communication infrastructure while remaining consistent with the visual language of Minecraft.

The conductor forms the continuous process line.

### 12.5 European historical layer

The lower scene progresses through several uses without becoming a chronological display:

early metalworking;

vessels and roofs;

water infrastructure;

electrical distribution;

communication;

public transport;

renewable systems;

repair and reuse.

The principal subject is not the monumentality of architecture. It is the network inside and around it.

### 12.6 Close-reading elements

Four visible copper states show progressive oxidation.

Lightning-rod forms alternate with roof profiles in the border.

A water channel connects several public buildings.

A restoration workshop reuses aged copper blocks.

Iron remains visible in rails, fastenings, hoppers, and frames.

The European map resembles an infrastructure network rather than a group of capitals.

### 12.7 Colour

Aged copper orange dominates.

Umber and warm paper provide structure. Verdigris is restricted to weathered surfaces, water reflections, and selected secondary lines.

The saturation remains moderate across both sides.

## 13. €100: Emerald

### 13.1 Civilisational function

Exchange and trust

### 13.2 Emerald or copper

A green copper denomination could be justified through oxidation and public architecture.

That choice would weaken two aspects of the series.

First, it would separate the material from its most immediate colour identity.

Second, it would remove emerald, which has a direct monetary role in Minecraft.

Emeralds function as the medium of exchange used in trading with villagers. Villagers have specialised professions and offer different goods or services. The system provides a clear visual model of trade, mutual dependence, valuation, and repeated exchange.

Emerald is therefore selected.

### 13.3 Rationale

Emerald has less structural or industrial importance than coal, copper, or iron.

That difference is productive.

Its value depends on rarity, appearance, recognised quality, skilled preparation, provenance, and confidence in exchange.

Currency also depends on collective recognition and confidence in the system that supports it.

The €100 note therefore examines value as a social relationship.

The question is not only what an emerald can physically do. The question is why people accept it in exchange for other forms of labour and material.

### 13.4 Recto

## 14. €200: Gold

### 14.1 Civilisational function

Reserve and continuity

### 14.2 Rationale

Gold concludes the series because it combines physical use, exchange value, ceremonial meaning, and long-term storage.

Its practical roles include reliable conduction, resistant contacts, reflective surfaces, precision instruments, and specialised medical and scientific applications.

Within Minecraft, gold has several distinct functions:

powered rails;

clocks;

golden food associated with healing;

beacons;

decorative construction;

barter with piglins.

Gold tools also have limited durability. The material is therefore not presented as universally superior.

The €200 note concerns the responsible direction of stored value.

A reserve is meaningful when it supports continuity, transport, treatment, research, and action beyond the immediate moment.

### 14.3 Recto

## 21. Evaluation summary

### Visual harmony and coherence

All six notes share one horizontal structure, one recto-verso narrative, one ore-lens system, one approach to typography, and one restrained print palette.

### Thematic consistency

Every denomination begins with a natural material and ends with its civilisational use.

The Minecraft mechanics are directly related to each material rather than applied as unrelated references.

### Visual appeal and aesthetic quality

Detailed engraving, ordered density, material texture, architecture, landscape, and restrained colour provide visual depth without sacrificing denomination recognition.

### European representation

Europe is represented through shared achievements and systems:

industry;

engineering;

education;

art;

public infrastructure;

trade;

law;

scientific inquiry;

navigation;

medicine;

long-term institutional capacity.

### Denomination discrimination

Each note has a distinct colour family, material, landscape, process line, border pattern, and tonal distribution.

### Public recognition

The large numeral and material narrative remain immediate. Familiarity with the game is not required.

### Accessibility

Dimensions, numerals, contrast, tactile reservations, patterns, and silhouettes support identification beyond colour alone.

### Creativity and originality

Minecraft is treated as a formal grammar of matter, transformation, and collective construction. The subject is integrated into the visual and conceptual structure of currency rather than added as a licensed image layer.

## 22. Closing statement

Crafting Europe presents value as a process.

The recto begins with a material whose future use is not yet determined.

The verso shows the systems through which that material becomes socially useful.

Coal becomes energy.
"Redstone" becomes coordination.
Lapis becomes knowledge and culture.
Copper becomes infrastructure.
Emerald becomes exchange.
Gold becomes continuity.

The sequence describes Europe not as a static collection of symbols, but as a shared world made through resources, learning, specialisation, maintenance, and cooperation.

A banknote performs the same transformation at a smaller scale.

Printed material becomes a bearer of value because a public system gives it form, recognition, and trust.
