# Living Systems

Version 3

## Euro banknote design concept

### Independent alternative thematic study assessed under the published design-contest framework

### Theme

Interdependent civic systems

### Narrative

The series follows how shared infrastructure enables communication, communication enables coordinated mobility, mobility broadens interpretation, interpretation creates belonging, belonging sustains environmental stewardship, and stewardship preserves the possibility of an open future.

### Denomination motifs

| Denomination | Motif | Representation channel | Series function |
| ------------ | ----- | ---------------------- | --------------- |

| €10 | Communication | Telecommunications | Exchange of signals across distance |

## 1. Executive summary

Living Systems presents Europe through six interdependent capacities that make collective life possible.

The series is deliberately not encyclopaedic. Each denomination names a broad system but represents it through one concrete channel. Infrastructure is represented through electricity. Communication is represented through telecommunications. Mobility is represented through rail. Interpretation is represented through theatre. Environment is represented through a garden on the recto and watershed stewardship on the verso. Future is represented through space exploration.

The representation channel is not claimed to exhaust the meaning of the motif. A train is not all mobility, and a telecommunications mast is not all communication. The purpose of the choice is visual clarity. Each note must have a principal noun before it has secondary meaning.

The six denominations form a dependency sequence rather than a hierarchy of importance.

Infrastructure enables communication because communication requires reliable energy, physical networks, buildings, maintenance, and shared standards.

Communication enables mobility because purposeful movement depends on signalling, coordination, routing, schedules, changing conditions, and the exchange of information across distance.

Mobility broadens interpretation because movement brings people, languages, experiences, and points of view into contact.

Interpretation creates belonging because culture gives places, histories, and communities shared meaning.

Belonging supports environmental stewardship because people are more likely to care for places they understand as meaningful and inherited.

Environmental stewardship preserves the future because ecological health and maintained public systems keep options open for generations that have not yet arrived.

The recto and verso have different narrative jobs.

The recto is emblematic. It shows the denomination's resolved immediate-scale manifestation, using the camera and spatial scale specified in that denomination chapter.

The verso is systemic. It reveals the larger infrastructure, institution, landscape, and chain of capability that make the recto possible. Across the series, the reverse normally shifts to an elevated viewpoint and reveals relationships that the recto cannot show. The reverse must not repeat the recto at a larger scale or add more objects to the same scene. It must be qualitatively different while remaining conceptually connected.

Visual hierarchy is a governing rule. Most scenes contain one dominant element and no more than one secondary element. Supporting detail exists only beneath that hierarchy and is subordinated by scale, contrast, placement, and lightness. The €100 is the deliberate exception because Environment is most legible as an interacting landscape rather than as a single object.

Human figures are absent from the current scene set except for the three small performer silhouettes specified on the €50 recto. No realistic person becomes a focal subject.

The image language is neither photorealistic nor cartoon-like. It is derived from clean architectural visualisation: matte surfaces, simplified geometry, controlled perspective, restrained atmospheric depth, soft lighting, and clear silhouettes. Frutiger Aero is inherited conceptually through optimism, environmental integration, clean technology, open atmosphere, and legible systems rather than through literal desktop imagery, skeuomorphism, glossy interface effects, or nostalgic imitation.

Colour is denomination-led and hue-uniform. Each note has one dominant colour family. Most modelling occurs through lightness and density rather than through changes of hue. Except for required institutional colours and near-neutral paper tones, secondary chromatic variation should remain within approximately a twelve-percent arc of the colour wheel around the denomination hue.

The governing requirement is:

> One clear image first; system meaning through structure; detail only after hierarchy is secure.

## 2. Theme, narrative, and motifs

### 2.1 Theme

The theme concerns the systems through which Europeans live together.

Infrastructure, communication, mobility, interpretation, environment, and future are not isolated policy areas. They are connected civic capacities. Each relies on systems that are distributed, maintained, and usually taken for granted when they work well.

Money is an appropriate carrier for this theme because currency is itself a shared system. A banknote exists through standards, recognition, circulation, trust, maintenance, and institutional continuity.

The European dimension lies in networks that cross borders without requiring a catalogue of national symbols. Electricity grids, telecommunications, railways, culture, ecosystems, and scientific ambitions operate locally while depending on wider cooperation.

The central tension is optimism and responsibility.

The proposal is optimistic in its spatial language: open skies, clean technical forms, maintained landscapes, legible infrastructure, and technology integrated with public life. That optimism is not automatic. Versoes reveal the material systems that make it possible.

### 2.2 Narrative

Across the series:

Infrastructure creates a reliable substrate.

Communication allows signals to travel across that substrate.

Mobility turns communication and coordination into purposeful physical movement.

Interpretation turns movement and encounter into meaning.

Environment turns belonging into stewardship.

Future extends stewardship into responsibility for possibilities not yet fixed.

The €50 marks the main conceptual change of register. The first three denominations concern physical substrate, signal exchange, and movement. The €50 asks what those capacities produce culturally.

The €100 then expands the object of care from human institutions to the landscapes and ecosystems within which they exist.

The €200 closes the sequence with aspiration rather than prediction.

Its question is:

> What can maintained systems allow future generations to attempt?

### 2.3 Representation channels

A broad motif is not an instruction to place every associated object in one scene.

Each denomination therefore has one chosen representation channel and one resolved image pair.

€5, Infrastructure

Infrastructure is represented through electricity. The recto shows transmission as the visible substrate of modern life. The verso reveals generation through an anonymous nuclear reactor-building complex.

€10, Communication

Communication is represented through telecommunications. The recto shows one terrestrial communications mast from ground level at a low angle. The verso shifts to a high elevated view over an anonymous radiocommunications site with one dominant satellite dish and exactly two secondary dishes.

€20, Mobility

Mobility is represented through rail. The recto is anchored by one sweeping curved railway bridge crossing a broad river. The verso shifts upward to a high oblique view of a major rail junction where four principal track paths converge and diverge.

€50, Interpretation

Interpretation is represented through theatre. The recto shows the public face of interpretation: a stage seen from the rear of a mostly empty auditorium. The verso moves behind the presentation into the backstage system that makes the performance possible: fly tower, rigging, lighting infrastructure, scenery handling, and technical circulation.

€100, Environment

Environment is represented through two scales of stewardship. The recto shows the experience of environment through a contemporary public garden organised around water and planting. The verso shifts to a high landscape view of the broader watershed system that sustains environmental quality, integrating renewable energy, water management, restoration, forestry, and agriculture.

€200, Future

Future is represented through space exploration. The recto shows an anonymous upper stage during orbital circularisation with Earth behind it. The verso shifts to an elevated terrestrial view of the space-centre infrastructure that makes the orbital scene possible.

### 2.4 Why these motifs

The motifs are selected for immediate visual legibility.

Power lines, a nuclear power station, a communications mast, satellite dishes, a curved railway bridge, a theatre stage, wind turbines, an orbital launcher, and a launch centre are all capable of producing strong silhouettes.

They also support qualitative difference between recto and verso.

The viewer does not need to decode a diagram or read explanatory text before understanding the principal image. Interpretation can deepen afterward.

The series therefore prefers a memorable concrete scene over a comprehensive but weak illustration of a category.

## 3. Assessment framework

Living Systems is structured against the published euro-banknote design framework.

The proposal retains the need for:

coherence across the €5, €10, €20, €50, €100, and €200;

clear distinction between denominations;

accessibility;

technical feasibility;

common European institutional elements;

consistency between theme and imagery;

provision for the European flag;

a stable map of Europe;

a signature area;

tactile identification;

later integration of security features.

The published visual and technical criteria are:

| Criterion | Published weighting |
| --------- | -------------------: |
| Visual harmony and coherence of the complete series | 10% |
| Thematic consistency with the story | 10% |
| Visual appeal and aesthetic quality | 20% |
| Symbolism and cultural representation of the EU | 10% |
| Discrimination between denominations | 10% |
| Public recognition | 5% |
| Accessibility | 10% |
| Creativity and originality | 25% |

The common system consists of:

a shared recto and verso grid;

a common humanist typographic system;

stable institutional fields;

narrow denomination-led colour families;

matte architectural visualisation;

consistent scene hierarchy;

a stable relationship between dominant motif, open space, and institutional information;

a common rule that the scene ends at the note boundary.

The principal variables are:

denomination proportion;

dominant hue;

recto subject;

verso subject;

silhouette;

border rhythm;

light-dark distribution;

tactile group.

Accessibility is supported through dimensions, numerals, contrast, silhouette, border pattern, tactile reservations, and scene type, not colour alone.

Security features are not presented as finished technical solutions. Only security-ready zones and transformable pattern opportunities are identified.

## 4. Conceptual basis

### 4.1 A living system as a design subject

The title Living Systems describes a model of public life rather than a biological theme.

A living system is connected, maintained, adaptive, and dependent on flows.

Electricity flows through grids.

Signals move through communication networks.

People and goods move through transport networks.

Culture moves through performance, preservation, and reinterpretation.

Water, energy, species, and human activity interact across landscapes.

Future capability depends on institutions that preserve knowledge, maintain infrastructure, and support long-term ambition.

The series therefore treats Europe as a set of relationships rather than a set of isolated monuments.

### 4.2 Frutiger Aero as conceptual inheritance

The proposal takes Frutiger Aero seriously as an optimistic worldview, not as a checklist of visual effects.

The relevant inheritance is:

technology integrated with everyday life;

clean public infrastructure;

open skies and atmospheric light;

water and vegetation as active parts of designed environments;

rounded but functional geometry;

legible humanist information design;

confidence that technological and environmental systems can coexist;

a bright, civic, forward-looking mood.

The proposal does not require:

skeuomorphism;

glossy glass buttons;

floating bubbles;

desktop wallpaper compositions;

operating-system references;

consumer electronics;

lens flare;

decorative chrome;

interface imitation.

Frutiger Aero is therefore conceptual rather than literal. Its optimism survives, while the imagery is translated into a disciplined banknote system.

### 4.3 Comprehension without prior knowledge

A viewer should be able to read each note without knowing the term Frutiger Aero.

€5: electricity is transmitted and generated.

€10: signals are transmitted across distance.

€20: rail creates coordinated physical mobility.

€50: culture is presented through performance and sustained by backstage technical systems.

€100: a cared-for garden belongs to a wider environmental system of water, land, energy, and restoration.

€200: exploration depends on terrestrial scientific and engineering capability.

The visual reference adds atmosphere, not meaning required for comprehension.

### 4.4 European scope and anonymity

The designs avoid named institutions and specific national branding.

Real sites, technologies, and architectural precedents inform the imagery, while the final scenes remain anonymous and non-identifiable.

A nuclear power station should not reproduce one identifiable plant.

The €10 radiocommunications site takes visual inspiration from European satellite-communication facilities without reproducing a protected landmark, company identity, national operator, logo, or exact site plan.

The €200 launcher recalls the proportions and engineering logic of European launch systems without carrying ESA, Ariane, company, national, or programme branding. Its purpose is to represent a durable category of capability rather than one institution frozen in time.

This anonymity follows a general monetary principle: the scene should remain meaningful even as real institutions, operators, technologies, and organisations evolve.

## 5. Visual reference and monetary precedent

### 5.1 Primary visual reference

The visual reference is early twenty-first-century architectural and civic visualisation interpreted through the optimistic logic associated with Frutiger Aero.

Useful characteristics include:

clean matte surfaces;

simple geometric massing;

soft environmental light;

clear air and water;

legible infrastructure;

controlled rounded forms;

open horizons;

environmental integration;

technical objects treated as civic rather than industrial spectacle.

### 5.2 Monetary translation

The scene should feel integrated into the banknote rather than pasted on top of it.

This does not mean imitating intaglio engraving.

Narrative scenes use tonal, architectural-render treatment with limited texture and simplified material behaviour. Banknote structure is provided by composition, typography, borders, numerals, institutional fields, subtle line patterns, and tonal integration.

The design avoids the assumption that monetary seriousness requires simulated nineteenth-century engraving.

### 5.3 What is not transferred

The proposal excludes:

literal software aesthetics;

skeuomorphic controls;

photoreal commercial rendering;

cartoon rendering;

painterly fantasy;

cinematic visual effects;

glossy product visualisation;

highly saturated eco-branding;

ornamental pseudo-security linework inside the narrative scene;

dense intaglio imitation.

## 6. Narrative structure

### 6.1 Recto role

The recto is emblematic.

It presents one concrete manifestation of the denomination's system.

The recto should normally contain:

one dominant element;

at most one secondary element;

a restrained environment;

large areas of visual rest.

The image must remain recognisable at thumbnail scale.

### 6.2 Verso role

The verso is systemic.

It answers:

What larger infrastructure or institution makes the recto possible?

How does the system operate beyond the single emblem?

What wider capacity does it create?

The verso is not a busier recto.

Across the series, the reverse normally changes both scale and camera position. Rectos stay close to an encounter, object, or experience. Versos move to an elevated oblique view that reveals the broader system and its relationships.

The resolved pairs are:

transmission towers become a nuclear reactor-building complex;

a ground-level communications mast becomes a high-angle radiocommunications site;

a curved rail bridge becomes an elevated railway junction;

a theatre stage becomes the backstage technical system behind it;

a public garden becomes a managed watershed landscape;

an orbital upper stage becomes a terrestrial space centre.

### 6.3 Series progression

€5 provides physical substrate.

€10 allows communication across distance.

€20 converts coordination into movement.

€50 converts encounter into interpretation.

€100 converts belonging into stewardship.

€200 converts stewardship into preserved possibility and ambition.

## 7. Scene hierarchy

### 7.1 Dominant-element rule

Every scene begins with one visual decision:

What is the dominant element?

If that answer cannot be stated in a short noun phrase, the scene is not ready.

The dominant element controls:

largest occupied area;

strongest silhouette;

highest local contrast;

principal directional rhythm;

main overlap;

first thumbnail reading.

Secondary elements exist only to clarify scale, context, and system relationship.

### 7.2 Maximum scene complexity

Most rectos contain:

one dominant element;

at most one named secondary element;

a restrained supporting environment;

minimal fine detail.

Versos have a larger complexity budget because their role is systemic. They can contain several subordinate technical components when those components belong to one resolved system. They still require one unmistakable centre of gravity.

No scene contains several equally important buildings, vehicles, devices, human activities, diagrams, and landscapes.

The image model must never have to guess what matters most.

### 7.3 Deliberate exception: €100

Environment is the one denomination designed around a coordinated living landscape rather than a single technical object.

This is not permission for a collage.

The recto remains simple: one public garden organised around a pond and one footbridge.

The verso is deliberately richer: one watershed landscape organised by a single river, with four wind turbines on one ridge, one compact solar field, restored wetland, reforestation, managed agriculture, and one low water-control structure.

Every element belongs to the same spatial system.

The €100 is therefore the most visually expansive and the most directly Frutiger-Aero denomination in the series.

### 7.4 Quiet space

Open sky, calm water, pale terrain, matte walls, and simple tonal fields are active compositional elements.

Empty space is not unfinished space.

It protects hierarchy and makes the dominant object readable.

### 7.5 Resolved scene specification

The proposal is a decision document, not an exploration document.

Every final scene description fixes:

the dominant subject;

the number and identity of secondary elements;

the camera height and viewing angle;

the spatial setting;

the main directional geometry;

the human-presence rule;

the colour family;

the lighting and rendering language;

the major exclusions.

Words that introduce alternatives, including “or”, “may”, “could”, “perhaps”, “either”, “possibly”, “around”, “such as”, and “preferably”, require review when they affect visible content. They remain valid in conceptual explanation, comparison, exclusion, and non-visual technical discussion. They are removed from final scene specifications when they create a choice that should already have been resolved.

A prompt must not ask the image model to choose its protagonist, setting, viewpoint, secondary structure, or scene type.

## 8. Visual rendering style

### 8.1 Target style

The narrative imagery uses a clean architectural-render visual language.

The target is:

deliberately simplified;

matte;

spatially coherent;

clean;

calm;

readable;

idealised but credible.

It should resemble a carefully art-directed architectural visualisation rather than photography, cartoon illustration, or painterly rendering.

### 8.2 Geometry

Forms are simplified without becoming toy-like.

Complex engineering structures retain enough detail to be credible, but small components are grouped into larger readable masses.

Edges are clean.

Perspective is controlled.

Surfaces do not require microscopic texture.

### 8.3 Materials

Materials are communicated primarily through lightness, edge behaviour, and restrained reflectivity.

Concrete is matte.

Metal is satin rather than mirror-like.

Glass is lightly translucent without interface-like glow.

Vegetation is simplified into mass and silhouette rather than individually rendered leaves.

Water is clean and calm unless movement is compositionally necessary.

### 8.4 Lighting

Lighting is soft and directional.

Scenes avoid dramatic cinematic contrast.

The mood is bright, lucid, and slightly idealised.

Shadows clarify form but do not dominate.

Highlights remain restrained.

### 8.5 Human presence

Realistic people are avoided.

The preferred order is:

empty scene;

small distant silhouettes;

simple anonymous figures used only for scale.

No faces are required.

No individual figure carries narrative meaning.

No crowd becomes a visual texture.

### 8.6 Relation to print

Scenes should feel embedded into the banknote through restricted colour, tonal integration, cropped composition, and common graphic structure.

They should not be converted into fake engraving.

Fine geometric border and security-ready patterns sit outside and around the narrative scene, while the principal image remains a matte architectural visualisation.

## 9. Monetary design principles

### 9.1 Visual authority

The notes must read as money before the theme is analysed.

Each design integrates:

a large denomination numeral;

EURO, ΕΥΡΩ, and ЕВРО;

the European flag;

a stable Europe map;

a signature field;

serial areas;

tactile reservations;

security-ready zones;

a controlled outer frame;

a denomination-specific pattern family;

a strong tonal hierarchy.

The monetary scaffold and narrative scene are composed together.

### 9.2 Two reading distances

From a distance, the viewer reads:

denomination;

physical proportion;

colour family;

dominant silhouette;

large light-dark pattern.

At close range, the viewer discovers:

technical structure;

small environmental relationships;

maintenance access;

signal and route logic;

border patterns;

map treatment;

secondary continuity cues.

### 9.3 Detail policy

Detail is permitted only after the dominant form is secure.

Close-reading detail must not create new focal points.

The proposal therefore rejects “detail as quantity.” Detail is valuable when it explains structure, material, maintenance, or use.

### 9.4 Horizontal use

Principal elements remain legible when the note is:

held horizontally;

partially covered;

stacked;

fanned;

viewed quickly during payment.

Large numerals remain visible near the short edges.

## 10. Colour policy

### 10.1 General rule

Each denomination is hue-uniform.

The note uses one dominant chromatic family with reasonable saturation.

Most visual differentiation occurs through:

lightness;

shadow;

density;

transparency;

near-neutral paper tones.

Except for required institutional colours and neutrals, chromatic variation should remain within approximately a twelve-percent arc of the colour wheel around the denomination hue.

This is a target rather than a mathematical production tolerance.

### 10.2 Why hue uniformity matters

A narrow hue family:

strengthens denomination recognition;

reduces competition inside the scene;

helps complex structures read as one printed object;

prevents the scene from becoming a colourful illustration pasted onto currency;

makes lightness carry hierarchy;

creates stronger family resemblance across recto and verso.

### 10.3 Denomination palettes

| Denomination | Dominant family | Main interpretation |
| ------------ | --------------- | ------------------- |
| €5 | Grey | cool pearl grey, graphite, concrete, silver-grey |
| €10 | Red | muted signal red, pale rose, warm red-grey |
| €20 | Blue | clear rail blue, pale sky-blue, slate-blue |
| €50 | Orange-brown | amber, terracotta, warm ochre-brown, cream |
| €100 | Green | fresh green, jade, pale green-cyan, moss-grey |
| €200 | Yellow-brown | solar ochre, warm yellow, cream, pale amber |

The €5 is predominantly achromatic with a small cool bias and no broad hue range.

### 10.4 Saturation

Saturation remains moderate.

The brightest chroma is reserved for:

principal numeral;

small route and signal accents;

selected structural edges;

specific environmental cues.

Large surfaces should not glow.

### 10.5 Grayscale performance

Each denomination remains distinguishable without colour.

€5: tall transmission rhythm and industrial generation mass.

€10: vertical mast and broad dish geometry.

€20: sweeping bridge curve and branching junction.

€50: stage aperture and vertical backstage rigging.

€100: garden water curve and watershed contour structure.

€200: orbital silhouette and launch-centre massing.

### 11.2 Recto composition

The recto uses three integrated zones rather than rigid panels:

a value zone near one short edge;

a dominant image field;

an institutional zone integrated into the remaining negative space.

The image overlaps the value and institutional zones in a controlled way while hierarchy remains clear.

### 11.3 Verso composition

The verso uses:

a value zone;

a broader systemic image field;

a compact institutional zone.

The verso image composition differs substantially from the recto whenever that difference is required to reveal the broader system.

Series coherence comes from typography, value placement, colour, borders, map treatment, and crop logic rather than from forcing the same scenic arrangement on both sides.

### 11.4 Shared alignments

principal numeral zone;

EURO / ΕΥΡΩ / ЕВРО hierarchy;

flag location family;

map field family;

tactile reservation;

### 11.6 Borders

Borders remain subordinate to the scene.

They derive from simple system geometries:

€5: transmission lines and grid rhythms;

€10: radio waves and antenna geometry;

€20: rail curves, points, and parallel lines;

€50: stage arcs, rigging lines, and backstage grid rhythms;

€100: wind, water, leaf, and contour lines;

€200: orbital arcs and launch trajectories.

Borders remain integrated into the monetary composition and never become an ornamental frame around the narrative scene.

## 12. Typography and institutional integration

### 12.1 Typography

The typography uses a custom humanist sans-serif direction inspired by early twenty-first-century public information design.

It should not literally reproduce a software interface typeface.

The principal numeral is broad, calm, and highly legible.

EURO, ΕΥΡΩ, and ЕВРО retain equal visual authority and a stable hierarchy.

### 12.2 Europe map

The Europe map uses one stable silhouette, orientation, and projection across the series.

Internal treatment varies by denomination:

€5 grid lines;

€10 radio-wave and connection arcs;

€20 rail paths;

€50 cultural circulation;

€100 environmental contours and watersheds;

€200 orbital and observation arcs.

The map is never replaced by a decorative blob or generated pseudo-map.

### 12.3 Institutional anonymity

Scene architecture and technology remain anonymous.

No company marks.

No operator logos.

No named research institutions.

No national transport branding.

No ESA branding.

No recognisable commercial interface.

## 13. Human presence

The current twelve scene specifications contain no people except on the €50 recto.

The €50 recto contains exactly three simplified performer silhouettes because performance requires a visible act of presentation. They remain tiny inside the stage aperture.

No other current prompt introduces a scale figure.

Figures never receive realistic faces, fashion detail, individual gestures, or portrait-like lighting.

No crowd becomes a visual texture.

This policy prevents generated people from becoming accidental protagonists and protects the architectural hierarchy.

| €10 | tall mast and large parabolic dish silhouettes |
| €20 | sweeping bridge curve and branching rail geometry |

## 15. Continuity and stewardship

Every system is shown as maintained rather than magically self-sustaining.

Infrastructure requires generation, transmission, inspection, and replacement.

Communication requires towers, dishes, physical sites, power, maintenance, and routing.

Mobility requires track, signalling, switching, inspection, and coordinated operation.

Interpretation requires performance, rehearsal, technical production, maintenance, access, and reinterpretation.

Environment requires restoration, monitoring, land management, and long-term care.

Future capability requires research, engineering, testing, maintenance, and preserved institutional knowledge.

Health remains cross-cutting rather than isolated as one denomination. Reliable infrastructure, communication, mobility, culture, environmental quality, and long-term research all affect human flourishing.

## 17. €5: Infrastructure

### 17.1 Series function

Foundation

The €5 represents the physical substrate on which the later systems depend.

Infrastructure is broader than electricity, but electricity is chosen as the representation channel because it is foundational to contemporary communication, transport, public services, research, and domestic life.

### 17.2 Rationale

Electricity gives the denomination a concrete and immediate visual vocabulary: transmission, generation, switching, and distribution.

The recto remains sparse because transmission is encountered as a line crossing landscape.

The verso uses the greater complexity budget of the reverse to reveal generation as a maintained industrial system rather than reducing nuclear power to one stereotyped cooling-tower silhouette.

### 17.3 Recto: transmission

The dominant element is one high-voltage transmission tower placed left of centre.

Two smaller towers recede diagonally toward the right distance and establish the continuation of the grid.

No substation appears on the recto.

The camera sits at ordinary landscape height with a level horizon, high enough to read the tower structure without turning it into a dramatic low-angle monument.

The ground is sparse and open. Vegetation is reduced to low tonal masses.

No people appear.

The image says:

electricity moves through a physical grid.

### 17.4 Verso: generation

The dominant subject is an anonymous nuclear reactor-building complex viewed from a high elevated oblique angle.

Two domed containment buildings form the central mass. A long turbine hall sits directly behind them. A compact switchyard occupies one side of the complex and connects to transmission lines leaving the site.

Service roads, cooling-water infrastructure, pipes, auxiliary buildings, and maintenance access are visible as subordinate parts of the same facility.

Cooling towers are not the compositional focus.

No workers appear.

The scene is calm, functional, maintained infrastructure. It avoids industrial dystopia and technological triumphalism.

### 17.5 Conceptual relationship

Recto: electricity transmitted.

Verso: electricity generated and organised at system scale.

The reverse expands the viewer's understanding rather than repeating the transmission scene with added detail.

### 17.6 Colour

Use cool pearl grey, graphite, concrete grey, silver-grey, and paper white.

The scene is modelled primarily through lightness.

## 18. €10: Communication

### 18.1 Series function

Connection

Communication is an active relationship between parts of a system and leads directly into coordinated mobility.

Infrastructure makes communication physically possible.

Communication allows signals to cross distance.

Mobility then converts coordination into physical movement.

### 18.2 Rationale

Telecommunications is the chosen representation channel because it turns a distributed process into legible physical objects without pretending that one building makes the internet happen.

The recto represents terrestrial radio communication through one mast.

The verso represents the broader radiocommunications system through a site of large parabolic antennas.

The two sides deliberately use opposite camera logic so the dish does not merely replace the mast while leaving the composition unchanged.

### 18.3 Recto: terrestrial communication

The dominant element is one telecommunications mast on a low open ridge.

The camera is placed close to the ground and looks upward at a pronounced low angle.

The mast rises through a large quiet sky field.

One small technical shelter sits at the base and remains visually minor.

No other antenna structure appears.

No people appear.

The scene says:

signals cross distance from a visible terrestrial node.

### 18.4 Verso: radiocommunications site

The dominant scene is an anonymous radiocommunications site viewed from a high elevated oblique angle, approximately thirty degrees downward.

One monumental parabolic satellite dish forms the principal subject.

Exactly two smaller parabolic dishes sit farther across the site on different bearings.

No people appear.

### 18.5 Conceptual relationship

Recto: one terrestrial communications node experienced from below.

The shift from low-angle verticality to high-angle site geometry is part of denomination identity.

## 19. €20: Mobility

### 19.1 Series function

Adaptation

The €20 represents purposeful physical movement.

Rail is the chosen representation channel because it turns movement, coordination, public access, infrastructure, and cross-border connection into a highly legible system.

### 19.2 Rationale

Mobility depends on communication.

Signals, routing, schedules, remote coordination, and changing conditions allow trains to move safely and purposefully rather than blindly.

The recto uses one curved railway bridge as a memorable act of crossing.

The verso reveals the routing system that coordinates movement beyond that single crossing.

mobility crosses space.

## 20. €50: Interpretation

### 20.1 Series function

Meaning

The €50 is the conceptual midpoint.

Mobility creates encounters among places, languages, experiences, and points of view.

Interpretation turns those encounters into culture, debate, and shared meaning.

### 20.2 Rationale

Theatre is the chosen representation channel because interpretation is not only preserved content. It is an act of presentation shaped by staging, technical systems, performers, space, and audience.

The recto shows the public face of interpretation.

The verso turns the card over and shows the machinery behind that presentation.

This pair is more direct than moving from performance to an unrelated archival institution.

### 20.3 Recto: presentation

The dominant element is a contemporary theatre stage seen from the rear of a mostly empty auditorium.

The rectangular stage aperture controls the composition.

One minimal abstract set occupies the stage.

## 21. €100: Environment

### 21.1 Series function

Stewardship

The €100 represents the living environmental systems within which every earlier denomination operates.

It is the broadest spatial note and the deliberate exception to the one-object rule.

### 21.2 Rationale

The recto should not be a simplified version of the verso.

The recto therefore shows the experience of a cared-for environment at human scale: a garden.

A garden is already stewardship made visible. Water, shade, vegetation, paths, maintenance, and cultivation are encountered directly without turning the image into untouched wilderness.

The verso then reveals the broader watershed, energy, restoration, forestry, agriculture, and water-management system that sustains environmental quality at landscape scale.

This denomination carries the strongest direct Frutiger-Aero character in the series because it joins clean water, vegetation, open atmosphere, and technology within one optimistic visual language.

### 21.3 Recto: public garden

The recto shows a contemporary public garden at eye level.

A gently curved pond occupies the centre and lower middle of the image and forms the main compositional line.

One slender footbridge crosses the narrowest part of the pond as the only architectural secondary element.

Layered planting, low trees, reeds, and clipped groundcover form broad green masses around the water.

A simple path enters from the lower left and follows the pond edge.

No pavilion, turbine, solar array, monitoring device, crowd, or realistic person appears.

The scene is intimate, calm, maintained, and sensorial.

### 21.4 Verso: watershed stewardship system

The verso shows one continuous managed watershed from a high elevated oblique viewpoint.

A river enters from the upper left, bends through a restored wetland in the centre, and exits toward the lower right. The river is the organising line for the entire composition.

Exactly four wind turbines stand along one distant ridge.

One compact solar field occupies a single terrace below the ridge.

A reforested slope occupies one side of the river. Managed agricultural land occupies the opposite side.

One low water-control structure spans a narrow section of the river upstream of the wetland.

Discreet monitoring equipment sits beside the wetland and remains subordinate.

No settlement, resort architecture, giant visitor centre, crowd, or advertising element appears.

The composition is richer than the other notes, but it remains one watershed rather than a catalogue of environmental symbols.

### 21.5 Conceptual relationship

Recto: environmental quality experienced as a garden.

Verso: the wider stewardship system that maintains water, land, habitat, energy, and resilience.

The reverse zooms out in both scale and camera height.

### 21.6 Colour

Use fresh green, jade, pale green-cyan, moss-grey, and near-white.

The recto and verso remain hue-uniform. Water is translated toward pale green-cyan rather than becoming a separate blue field.

## 22. €200: Future

### 22.1 Series function

Possibility

The €200 closes the sequence with ambition rather than certainty.

Future is broader than space exploration, but space is selected as the representation channel because it is immediate, intuitive, and capable of expressing exploration, perspective, distance, technical ambition, and the unknown.

The scene does not predict that Europe's future is “space.”

It uses space exploration as a readable image of future-facing collective capability.

### 22.2 Rationale

The recto represents the dream and perspective of exploration.

The verso represents the terrestrial system that makes the dream technically possible.

The two sides deliberately separate aspiration from infrastructure.

Cross-denomination continuity is carried by the dependency narrative and the common visual grammar.

The €5 power system provides the substrate for the €10 communications system.

The €10 communications system provides the coordination logic visible in the €20 railway junction.

The €20's movement creates the encounters interpreted through the €50 theatre.

The €100 preserves environmental conditions inherited by the €200 future.

Do not add cameo objects solely to signal these links. The relationships are carried by the sequence itself.

## 29. Evaluation summary

### 29.1 Visual harmony and coherence

All six notes share:

a horizontal monetary structure;

matte architectural visualisation;

restricted human presence;

denomination-led hue families;

stable institutional elements;

strong silhouette hierarchy;

controlled quiet space;

consistent crop discipline.

Coherence comes from common design grammar rather than identical scene construction.

### 29.2 Thematic consistency

Every denomination names a broad system and selects one concrete representation channel.

The recto is emblematic.

The verso is systemic.

The six roles form one functional sequence from physical substrate to open future.

### 29.3 Visual appeal and aesthetic quality

The revision prioritises clear dominant forms over scene quantity.

Each side should remain visually understandable before close details are inspected.

The €100 recto is intentionally intimate and simple; the €100 verso is intentionally richer, with all complexity organised by one continuous watershed.

### 29.4 Symbolism and cultural representation of the EU

The series represents Europe through shared capability:

electricity infrastructure;

telecommunications;

rail mobility;

culture and theatrical production;

environmental stewardship;

future-facing research and exploration.

The imagery remains anonymous enough to outlive specific operators and institutions.

### 29.5 Denomination discrimination

Independent signals include:

physical proportion;

colour family;

dominant recto silhouette;

dominant verso silhouette;

border rhythm;

tonal distribution;

tactile group;

spatial type.

### 29.6 Public recognition

The revised scenes use concrete objects rather than abstract institutional composites.

Transmission towers, satellite dishes, railway bridges, theatre stages, public gardens, launch vehicles, and resolved technical facilities are easier to recognise than generic campuses and mixed-function complexes.

### 29.7 Accessibility

Large numerals, strong local contrast, tactile reservations, denomination-specific silhouettes, grayscale separation, and simplified scene hierarchy support accessibility.

Final accessibility performance requires specialist testing.

### 29.8 Creativity and originality

The proposal translates the optimistic worldview associated with Frutiger Aero into a restrained monetary and architectural language.

Its originality lies less in surface decoration than in using clean technological and environmental imagery to construct a dependency narrative across six denominations.

## 30. Closing statement

Living Systems begins with the physical substrate of modern life and ends with an object leaving Earth.

Between those points, electricity enables communication, communication enables coordinated movement, movement broadens interpretation, interpretation produces belonging, belonging motivates stewardship, and stewardship preserves the ability to attempt what does not yet exist.

The series does not try to show everything each concept can mean.

It makes choices.

One denomination, one broad system, one concrete representation channel.

Most scenes have one dominant element.

The €100 alone becomes deliberately plural because environment is itself a network of relationships.

The result is intended to feel optimistic without becoming nostalgic, technological without becoming corporate, environmental without becoming promotional, and futuristic without becoming speculative spectacle.

The notes present public capability as something built, maintained, shared, and inherited.

## Why the revision was necessary

Version 1 defined the conceptual taxonomy more successfully than it defined the images.

Its main visual failure was not lack of detail but lack of hierarchy. Several scenes attempted to represent all aspects of a motif simultaneously, producing generic campuses, mixed institutions, transport catalogues, and research landscapes without a clear dominant subject.

Version 2 converts each abstract category into a chosen visual channel and gives each side a concrete compositional anchor.

The revision therefore moves the proposal from “illustrate the full definition of the system” to “choose one image that makes the system immediately legible.”

That principle is now the basis for all further design generation.

## Why this revision was necessary

Version 2 fixed the major problem of scene overload but still contained unresolved alternatives inside nominally final image descriptions.

Those alternatives were small in prose and large in generation. A choice between bridge and viaduct, river and valley, rear and middle auditorium, archive and preservation hall, one and two secondary dishes, or launcher and upper stage changes composition, silhouette, density, camera logic, and often the entire visual reading.

Version 3 treats the proposal as the place where those choices are resolved. The image model still interprets form, atmosphere, and surface detail, but it no longer chooses the scene concept on behalf of the design proposal.
