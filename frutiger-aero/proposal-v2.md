## 1. Executive summary

Living Systems presents Europe through six interdependent capacities that make collective life possible.

The series is deliberately not encyclopaedic. Each denomination names a broad system but represents it through one concrete channel. Infrastructure is represented through electricity. Communication is represented through telecommunications. Mobility is represented through rail. Interpretation is represented through theatre and cultural memory. Environment is represented through an integrated landscape of ecology and renewable systems. Future is represented through space exploration.

The representation channel is not claimed to exhaust the meaning of the motif. A train is not all mobility, and a telecommunications mast is not all communication. The purpose of the choice is visual clarity. Each note must have a principal noun before it has secondary meaning.

The six denominations form a dependency sequence rather than a hierarchy of importance.

Infrastructure enables communication because communication requires reliable energy, physical networks, buildings, maintenance, and shared standards.

Communication enables mobility because purposeful movement depends on signalling, coordination, routing, schedules, changing conditions, and the exchange of information across distance.

Mobility broadens interpretation because movement brings people, languages, experiences, and points of view into contact.

Interpretation creates belonging because culture gives places, histories, and communities shared meaning.

Belonging supports environmental stewardship because people are more likely to care for places they understand as meaningful and inherited.

Environmental stewardship preserves the future because ecological health and maintained public systems keep options open for generations that have not yet arrived.

The recto and verso have different narrative jobs.

The recto is emblematic. It shows one immediately readable manifestation of the denomination's system.

The verso is systemic. It reveals the larger infrastructure, institution, landscape, or chain of capability that makes the recto possible. The reverse must not merely repeat the recto at a larger scale or add more objects to the same scene. It should be qualitatively different while remaining conceptually connected.

Visual hierarchy is a governing rule. Most scenes contain one dominant element and no more than one secondary element. Supporting detail may exist, but it is subordinated by scale, contrast, placement, and lightness. The €100 is the deliberate exception because Environment is most legible as an interacting landscape rather than as a single object.

Human figures are normally absent. Where scale or public use requires them, they appear only as small silhouettes or reduced anonymous forms. No realistic person becomes a focal subject.

The image language is neither photorealistic nor cartoon-like. It is derived from clean architectural visualisation: matte surfaces, simplified geometry, controlled perspective, restrained atmospheric depth, soft lighting, and clear silhouettes. Frutiger Aero is inherited conceptually through optimism, environmental integration, clean technology, open atmosphere, and legible systems rather than through literal desktop imagery, skeuomorphism, glossy interface effects, or nostalgic imitation.

Colour is denomination-led and hue-uniform. Each note has one dominant colour family. Most modelling occurs through lightness and density rather than through changes of hue. Except for required institutional colours and near-neutral paper tones, secondary chromatic variation should remain within approximately a twelve-percent arc of the colour wheel around the denomination hue.

The governing requirement is:

> One clear image first; system meaning through structure; detail only after hierarchy is secure.

## 2. Theme, narrative, and motifs

### 2.1 Theme

The theme concerns the systems through which Europeans live together.

Infrastructure, communication, mobility, interpretation, environment, and future are not isolated policy areas. They are connected civic capacities. Each relies on systems that are distributed, maintained, and usually taken for granted when they work well.

Money is an appropriate carrier for this theme because currency is itself a shared system. A banknote exists through standards, recognition, circulation, trust, maintenance, and institutional continuity.

The European dimension lies in networks that cross borders without requiring a catalogue of national symbols. Electricity grids, telecommunications, railways, culture, ecosystems, and scientific ambitions operate locally while depending on wider cooperation.

The central tension is optimism and responsibility.

The proposal is optimistic in its spatial language: open skies, clean technical forms, maintained landscapes, legible infrastructure, and technology integrated with public life. That optimism is not automatic. Versoes reveal the material systems that make it possible.

### 2.2 Narrative

Across the series:

Infrastructure creates a reliable substrate.

Communication allows signals to travel across that substrate.

Mobility turns communication and coordination into purposeful physical movement.

Interpretation turns movement and encounter into meaning.

Environment turns belonging into stewardship.

Future extends stewardship into responsibility for possibilities not yet fixed.

The €50 marks the main conceptual change of register. The first three denominations concern physical substrate, signal exchange, and movement. The €50 asks what those capacities produce culturally.

The €100 then expands the object of care from human institutions to the landscapes and ecosystems within which they exist.

The €200 closes the sequence with aspiration rather than prediction.

Its question is:

> What can maintained systems allow future generations to attempt?

### 2.3 Representation channels

A broad motif is not an instruction to place every associated object in one scene.

Each denomination therefore has one chosen representation channel.

€5, Infrastructure

Infrastructure is represented through electricity. The recto shows transmission as the visible substrate of modern life. The verso shows generation through a nuclear power station.

€10, Communication

Communication is represented through telecommunications. The recto shows a terrestrial communications mast. The verso shows an anonymous radiocommunications site dominated by large satellite dishes.

€20, Mobility

Mobility is represented through rail. The recto is anchored by a sweeping curved rail bridge. The verso shows the coordination of movement through a major rail junction.

€50, Interpretation

Interpretation is represented through cultural performance and cultural memory. The recto uses a theatre stage as the public act of interpretation. The verso shifts to the archive and preservation systems that allow culture to be revisited and reinterpreted.

€100, Environment

Environment is represented as an integrated living landscape. It is the series exception to the one-object principle. Wind energy, water, vegetation, restoration, and land use coexist because the subject is the relationship among systems rather than one isolated machine or ecosystem.

€200, Future

Future is represented through space exploration. The recto shows an anonymous European launcher during orbital circularisation with Earth behind it. The verso shows the terrestrial launch and assembly infrastructure that makes such exploration possible.

### 2.4 Why these motifs

The motifs are selected for immediate visual legibility.

Power lines, a nuclear power station, a communications mast, satellite dishes, a curved railway bridge, a theatre stage, wind turbines, an orbital launcher, and a launch centre are all capable of producing strong silhouettes.

They also support qualitative difference between recto and verso.

The viewer does not need to decode a diagram or read explanatory text before understanding the principal image. Interpretation can deepen afterward.

The series therefore prefers a memorable concrete scene over a comprehensive but weak illustration of a category.

## 3. Assessment framework

Living Systems is structured against the published euro-banknote design framework.

The proposal retains the need for:

coherence across the €5, €10, €20, €50, €100, and €200;

clear distinction between denominations;

accessibility;

technical feasibility;

common European institutional elements;

consistency between theme and imagery;

provision for the European flag;

a stable map of Europe;

a signature area;

tactile identification;

later integration of security features.

The published visual and technical criteria are:

| Criterion | Published weighting |
| --------- | -------------------: |
| Visual harmony and coherence of the complete series | 10% |
| Thematic consistency with the story | 10% |
| Visual appeal and aesthetic quality | 20% |
| Symbolism and cultural representation of the EU | 10% |
| Discrimination between denominations | 10% |
| Public recognition | 5% |
| Accessibility | 10% |
| Creativity and originality | 25% |

The common system consists of:

a shared recto and verso grid;

a common humanist typographic system;

stable institutional fields;

narrow denomination-led colour families;

matte architectural visualisation;

consistent scene hierarchy;

a stable relationship between dominant motif, open space, and institutional information;

a common rule that the scene ends at the note boundary.

The principal variables are:

denomination proportion;

dominant hue;

recto subject;

verso subject;

silhouette;

border rhythm;

light-dark distribution;

tactile group.

Accessibility is supported through dimensions, numerals, contrast, silhouette, border pattern, tactile reservations, and scene type, not colour alone.

Security features are not presented as finished technical solutions. Only security-ready zones and transformable pattern opportunities are identified.

## 4. Conceptual basis

### 4.1 A living system as a design subject

The title Living Systems describes a model of public life rather than a biological theme.

A living system is connected, maintained, adaptive, and dependent on flows.

Electricity flows through grids.

Signals move through communication networks.

People and goods move through transport networks.

Culture moves through performance, preservation, and reinterpretation.

Water, energy, species, and human activity interact across landscapes.

Future capability depends on institutions that preserve knowledge, maintain infrastructure, and support long-term ambition.

The series therefore treats Europe as a set of relationships rather than a set of isolated monuments.

### 4.2 Frutiger Aero as conceptual inheritance

The proposal takes Frutiger Aero seriously as an optimistic worldview, not as a checklist of visual effects.

The relevant inheritance is:

technology integrated with everyday life;

clean public infrastructure;

open skies and atmospheric light;

water and vegetation as active parts of designed environments;

rounded but functional geometry;

legible humanist information design;

confidence that technological and environmental systems can coexist;

a bright, civic, forward-looking mood.

The proposal does not require:

skeuomorphism;

glossy glass buttons;

floating bubbles;

desktop wallpaper compositions;

operating-system references;

consumer electronics;

lens flare;

decorative chrome;

interface imitation.

Frutiger Aero is therefore conceptual rather than literal. Its optimism survives, while the imagery is translated into a disciplined banknote system.

### 4.3 Comprehension without prior knowledge

A viewer should be able to read each note without knowing the term Frutiger Aero.

€5: electricity is transmitted and generated.

€10: signals are transmitted across distance.

€20: rail creates coordinated physical mobility.

€50: culture is performed, preserved, and reinterpreted.

€100: environmental stewardship depends on interacting natural and technical systems.

€200: exploration depends on terrestrial scientific and engineering capability.

The visual reference adds atmosphere, not meaning required for comprehension.

### 4.4 European scope and anonymity

The designs avoid named institutions and specific national branding.

Real sites, technologies, and architectural precedents may inform the imagery, but the final scenes remain anonymous.

A nuclear power station should not reproduce one identifiable plant.

The €10 radiocommunications site may take visual inspiration from European satellite-communication facilities, but it should not reproduce a protected landmark, company identity, national operator, logo, or exact site plan.

The €200 launcher may recall European launch systems but must not carry ESA, Ariane, company, national, or programme branding. Its purpose is to represent a durable category of capability rather than one institution frozen in time.

This anonymity follows a general monetary principle: the scene should remain meaningful even as real institutions, operators, technologies, and organisations evolve.

## 5. Visual reference and monetary precedent

### 5.1 Primary visual reference

The visual reference is early twenty-first-century architectural and civic visualisation interpreted through the optimistic logic associated with Frutiger Aero.

Useful characteristics include:

clean matte surfaces;

simple geometric massing;

soft environmental light;

clear air and water;

legible infrastructure;

controlled rounded forms;

open horizons;

environmental integration;

technical objects treated as civic rather than industrial spectacle.

### 5.2 Monetary translation

The scene should feel integrated into the banknote rather than pasted on top of it.

This does not mean imitating intaglio engraving.

Narrative scenes use tonal, architectural-render treatment with limited texture and simplified material behaviour. Banknote structure is provided by composition, typography, borders, numerals, institutional fields, subtle line patterns, and tonal integration.

The design avoids the assumption that monetary seriousness requires simulated nineteenth-century engraving.

### 5.3 What is not transferred

The proposal excludes:

literal software aesthetics;

skeuomorphic controls;

photoreal commercial rendering;

cartoon rendering;

painterly fantasy;

cinematic visual effects;

glossy product visualisation;

highly saturated eco-branding;

ornamental pseudo-security linework inside the narrative scene;

dense intaglio imitation.

## 6. Narrative structure

### 6.1 Recto role

The recto is emblematic.

It presents one concrete manifestation of the denomination's system.

The recto should normally contain:

one dominant element;

at most one secondary element;

a restrained environment;

large areas of visual rest.

The image must remain recognisable at thumbnail scale.

### 6.2 Verso role

The verso is systemic.

It answers:

What larger infrastructure or institution makes the recto possible?

How does the system operate beyond the single emblem?

What wider capacity does it create?

The verso is not a busier recto.

It should introduce a different spatial type, scale, or operation.

Examples:

transmission lines become a nuclear power station;

a communications mast becomes a satellite communications site;

a rail bridge becomes a rail junction;

a theatre stage becomes an archive;

an emblematic environmental landscape becomes a wider interacting stewardship landscape;

an orbital launcher becomes a terrestrial launch and assembly centre.

### 6.3 Series progression

€5 provides physical substrate.

€10 allows communication across distance.

€20 converts coordination into movement.

€50 converts encounter into interpretation.

€100 converts belonging into stewardship.

€200 converts stewardship into preserved possibility and ambition.

## 7. Scene hierarchy

### 7.1 Dominant-element rule

Every scene begins with one visual decision:

What is the dominant element?

If that answer cannot be stated in a short noun phrase, the scene is not ready.

The dominant element controls:

largest occupied area;

strongest silhouette;

highest local contrast;

principal directional rhythm;

main overlap;

first thumbnail reading.

Secondary elements exist only to clarify scale, context, or system relationship.

### 7.2 Maximum scene complexity

Most scenes contain:

one dominant element;

zero or one secondary element;

supporting landscape or architecture;

minimal fine detail.

No scene should contain several equally important buildings, vehicles, devices, human activities, diagrams, and landscapes.

The image model should never have to guess what matters most.

### 7.3 Deliberate exception: €100

Environment is the one denomination allowed to contain several coordinated objects.

This is not permission for a collage.

The dominant element is the landscape itself.

Wind turbines, water, vegetation, solar surfaces, restoration structures, and land management may coexist when they are composed as parts of one continuous environmental system.

The €100 is therefore the most visually expansive and the most directly Frutiger-Aero denomination in the series.

### 7.4 Quiet space

Open sky, calm water, pale terrain, matte walls, and simple tonal fields are active compositional elements.

Empty space is not unfinished space.

It protects hierarchy and makes the dominant object readable.

## 8. Visual rendering style

### 8.1 Target style

The narrative imagery uses a clean architectural-render visual language.

The target is:

deliberately simplified;

matte;

spatially coherent;

clean;

calm;

readable;

idealised but credible.

It should resemble a carefully art-directed architectural visualisation more than a photograph or illustration.

### 8.2 Geometry

Forms are simplified without becoming toy-like.

Complex engineering structures retain enough detail to be credible, but small components are grouped into larger readable masses.

Edges are clean.

Perspective is controlled.

Surfaces do not require microscopic texture.

### 8.3 Materials

Materials are communicated primarily through lightness, edge behaviour, and restrained reflectivity.

Concrete is matte.

Metal is satin rather than mirror-like.

Glass is clear or lightly translucent without interface-like glow.

Vegetation is simplified into mass and silhouette rather than individually rendered leaves.

Water is clean and calm unless movement is compositionally necessary.

### 8.4 Lighting

Lighting is soft and directional.

Scenes avoid dramatic cinematic contrast.

The mood is bright, lucid, and slightly idealised.

Shadows clarify form but do not dominate.

Highlights remain restrained.

### 8.5 Human presence

Realistic people are avoided.

The preferred order is:

empty scene;

small distant silhouettes;

simple anonymous figures used only for scale.

No faces are required.

No individual figure carries narrative meaning.

No crowd becomes a visual texture.

### 8.6 Relation to print

Scenes should feel embedded into the banknote through restricted colour, tonal integration, cropped composition, and common graphic structure.

They should not be converted into fake engraving.

The banknote may use fine geometric border and security-ready patterns around the scene, but the principal image remains a matte architectural visualisation.

## 9. Monetary design principles

### 9.1 Visual authority

The notes must read as money before the theme is analysed.

Each design integrates:

a large denomination numeral;

EURO, ΕΥΡΩ, and ЕВРО;

the European flag;

a stable Europe map;

a signature field;

serial areas;

tactile reservations;

security-ready zones;

a controlled outer frame;

a denomination-specific pattern family;

a strong tonal hierarchy.

The monetary scaffold and narrative scene are composed together.

### 9.2 Two reading distances

From a distance, the viewer reads:

denomination;

physical proportion;

colour family;

dominant silhouette;

large light-dark pattern.

At close range, the viewer discovers:

technical structure;

small environmental relationships;

maintenance access;

signal or route logic;

border patterns;

map treatment;

secondary continuity cues.

### 9.3 Detail policy

Detail is permitted only after the dominant form is secure.

Close-reading detail must not create new focal points.

The proposal therefore rejects “detail as quantity.” Detail is valuable when it explains structure, material, maintenance, or use.

### 9.4 Horizontal use

Principal elements remain legible when the note is:

held horizontally;

partially covered;

stacked;

fanned;

viewed quickly during payment.

Large numerals remain visible near the short edges.

## 10. Colour policy

### 10.1 General rule

Each denomination is hue-uniform.

The note uses one dominant chromatic family with reasonable saturation.

Most visual differentiation occurs through:

lightness;

shadow;

density;

transparency;

near-neutral paper tones.

Except for required institutional colours and neutrals, chromatic variation should remain within approximately a twelve-percent arc of the colour wheel around the denomination hue.

This is a target rather than a mathematical production tolerance.

### 10.2 Why hue uniformity matters

A narrow hue family:

strengthens denomination recognition;

reduces competition inside the scene;

helps complex structures read as one printed object;

prevents the scene from becoming a colourful illustration pasted onto currency;

makes lightness carry hierarchy;

creates stronger family resemblance across recto and verso.

### 10.3 Denomination palettes

| Denomination | Dominant family | Main interpretation |
| ------------ | --------------- | ------------------- |
| €5 | Grey | cool pearl grey, graphite, concrete, silver-grey |
| €10 | Red | muted signal red, pale rose, warm red-grey |
| €20 | Blue | clear rail blue, pale sky-blue, slate-blue |
| €50 | Orange-brown | amber, terracotta, warm ochre-brown, cream |
| €100 | Green | fresh green, jade, pale green-cyan, moss-grey |
| €200 | Yellow-brown | solar ochre, warm yellow, cream, pale amber |

The €5 is predominantly achromatic and may use a small cool bias rather than a broad hue range.

### 10.4 Saturation

Saturation remains moderate.

The brightest chroma is reserved for:

principal numeral;

small route or signal accents;

selected structural edges;

specific environmental cues.

Large surfaces should not glow.

### 10.5 Grayscale performance

Each denomination remains distinguishable without colour.

€5: tall transmission rhythm and industrial generation mass.

€10: vertical mast and broad dish geometry.

€20: sweeping bridge curve and branching junction.

€50: stage aperture and deep archive aisle.

€100: layered landscape and turbine rhythm.

€200: orbital silhouette and launch-centre massing.

## 11. Series-wide composition

### 11.1 Format and proportion

The six notes retain denomination-specific horizontal euro proportions.

| Denomination | Approximate working ratio |
| ------------ | ------------------------: |
| €5 | 1.94:1 |
| €10 | 1.90:1 |
| €20 | 1.85:1 |
| €50 | 1.82:1 |
| €100 | 1.91:1 |
| €200 | 1.99:1 |

A 1.9:1 master frame may be used during early composition.

The image ends at the note edge.

Presentation margins, photographed-paper backgrounds, rotation, perspective skew, and drop shadows are excluded from the design plate.

### 11.2 Recto composition

The recto uses three integrated zones rather than rigid panels:

a value zone near one short edge;

a dominant image field;

an institutional zone integrated into the remaining negative space.

The image may overlap the value and institutional zones, but hierarchy remains clear.

### 11.3 Verso composition

The verso uses:

a value zone;

a broader systemic image field;

a compact institutional zone.

The image composition is allowed to differ more substantially from the recto than in Version 1.

Series coherence comes from typography, value placement, colour, borders, map treatment, and crop logic rather than from forcing the same scenic arrangement on both sides.

### 11.4 Shared alignments

The following remain stable:

principal numeral zone;

EURO / ΕΥΡΩ / ЕВРО hierarchy;

flag location family;

map field family;

signature zone;

serial zones;

tactile reservation;

outer frame datum.

### 11.5 Fixed and variable elements

| Remains fixed | Changes by denomination |
| ------------- | ----------------------- |
| horizontal orientation | proportion |
| numeral hierarchy | dominant hue |
| institutional element family | recto composition |
| Europe-map geometry | verso composition |
| typographic family | dominant silhouette |
| tactile reservation logic | spatial depth |

### 11.6 Borders

Borders remain subordinate to the scene.

They derive from simple system geometries:

€5: transmission lines and grid rhythms;

€10: radio waves and antenna geometry;

€20: rail curves, points, and parallel lines;

€50: stage arcs, page edges, and archive rhythms;

€100: wind, water, leaf, and contour lines;

€200: orbital arcs and launch trajectories.

No border should form an unrequested decorative frame around the entire scene.

## 12. Typography and institutional integration

### 12.1 Typography

The typography uses a custom humanist sans-serif direction inspired by early twenty-first-century public information design.

It should not literally reproduce a software interface typeface.

The principal numeral is broad, calm, and highly legible.

EURO, ΕΥΡΩ, and ЕВРО retain equal visual authority and a stable hierarchy.

### 12.2 Europe map

The Europe map uses one stable silhouette, orientation, and projection across the series.

Internal treatment varies by denomination:

€5 grid lines;

€10 radio or connection arcs;

€20 rail paths;

€50 cultural circulation;

€100 environmental contours and watersheds;

€200 orbital and observation arcs.

The map is never replaced by a decorative blob or generated pseudo-map.

### 12.3 Institutional anonymity

Scene architecture and technology remain anonymous.

No company marks.

No operator logos.

No named research institutions.

No national transport branding.

No ESA branding.

No recognisable commercial interface.

## 13. Human presence

Human presence is optional rather than mandatory.

The default is an empty or nearly empty scene.

Where figures improve scale or public meaning, they appear as:

small silhouettes;

distant reduced forms;

one or two anonymous users.

Figures do not receive realistic faces, fashion detail, or portrait-like lighting.

This policy prevents generated people from becoming accidental protagonists and protects the architectural hierarchy.

## 14. Accessibility and denomination discrimination

Each denomination has independent non-colour signals.

| Denomination | Non-colour identification |
| ------------ | ------------------------- |
| €5 | repeated high-voltage verticals and industrial cooling-tower mass |
| €10 | tall mast and large parabolic dish silhouettes |
| €20 | sweeping bridge curve and branching rail geometry |
| €50 | rectangular stage aperture and deep archival perspective |
| €100 | turbine rhythm and layered landscape contours |
| €200 | diagonal orbital vehicle and large launch-centre geometry |

The tactile reservation near the short edges increases by denomination in a consistent progression.

The principal numeral remains legible under partial occlusion, reduced size, low contrast, and grayscale.

## 15. Continuity and stewardship

Every system is shown as maintained rather than magically self-sustaining.

Infrastructure requires generation, transmission, inspection, and replacement.

Communication requires towers, dishes, physical sites, power, maintenance, and routing.

Mobility requires track, signalling, switching, inspection, and coordinated operation.

Interpretation requires performance, preservation, conservation, access, and reinterpretation.

Environment requires restoration, monitoring, land management, and long-term care.

Future capability requires research, engineering, testing, maintenance, and preserved institutional knowledge.

Health remains cross-cutting rather than isolated as one denomination. Reliable infrastructure, communication, mobility, culture, environmental quality, and long-term research all affect human flourishing.

## 17. €5: Infrastructure

### 17.1 Series function

Foundation

The €5 represents the physical substrate on which the later systems depend.

Infrastructure is broader than electricity, but electricity is chosen as the representation channel because it is foundational to contemporary communication, transport, public services, research, and domestic life.

### 17.2 Rationale

The previous public-square and water-service concept was too diffuse.

Electricity gives the denomination a clear visual vocabulary:

transmission towers;

cables;

substations;

generation.

The note can therefore communicate Infrastructure without trying to display water, sanitation, streets, waste, lighting, planting, maintenance, and public space simultaneously.

### 17.3 Recto: transmission

The dominant element is a line of high-voltage transmission towers crossing a sparse landscape.

One tower may occupy the foreground and establish the main silhouette. One or two receding towers create direction and scale.

A compact substation may appear as the only secondary element, positioned low in the composition.

No people are required.

The landscape remains restrained and largely empty.

The scene should feel infrastructural rather than pastoral.

The purpose is immediate:

electricity moves through a physical grid.

### 17.4 Verso: generation

The dominant subject is an anonymous nuclear power station.

The plant is not a portrait of a specific real facility.

The composition should be built around one strong industrial mass, preferably a pair of cooling towers or a reactor-building complex depending on the chosen final silhouette.

A switchyard or transmission connection may appear as the one secondary element.

Service roads, pipes, auxiliary buildings, and water infrastructure remain subordinate.

No workers are required unless a tiny silhouette is useful for scale.

The scene should avoid both industrial dystopia and triumphalist technological spectacle.

It is calm, functional, maintained infrastructure.

### 17.5 Conceptual relationship

Recto: electricity transmitted.

Verso: electricity generated.

The distinction is simple enough to understand without text and broad enough to support the Infrastructure motif.

### 17.6 Colour

The €5 is the most neutral denomination.

Use cool pearl grey, graphite, concrete grey, silver-grey, and paper white.

If a chromatic bias is needed, keep it subtle and cool.

The scene is modeled primarily through lightness.

### 17.7 Prompt direction

Recto prompt:

Create the recto scene for a horizontal €5 Living Systems euro banknote concept. Infrastructure is represented through electricity. Make one high-voltage transmission tower the dominant element, with a small number of receding pylons crossing a sparse open landscape. A compact substation may appear as the only secondary element. Use a clean matte architectural-visualisation style, simplified credible geometry, soft daylight, controlled perspective, restrained atmospheric depth, and almost no people. Keep the palette hue-uniform in cool pearl grey and graphite, with most modelling through lightness. The scene must feel integrated into a banknote, not photorealistic, not cartoon-like, and not simulated intaglio. Preserve substantial quiet space. Exclude decorative frames, busy utility catalogues, crowds, branding, dramatic industrial smoke, neon effects, and cinematic spectacle.

Verso prompt:

Create the verso scene for a horizontal €5 Living Systems euro banknote concept. Show an anonymous nuclear power station as the unmistakable dominant subject. Use one strong generation silhouette, such as a pair of cooling towers with a restrained reactor-building mass, plus a small switchyard or transmission connection as the only secondary element. Keep surrounding service infrastructure quiet and subordinate. Use a clean matte architectural-render style, simplified credible engineering, soft controlled light, and a cool pearl-grey monochromatic family modeled mainly through lightness. No logos, no named plant, no realistic people, no smoke-heavy dystopian mood, no exaggerated futuristic technology, no intaglio imitation, and no decorative outer frame.

## 18. €10: Communication

### 18.1 Series function

Connection

The €10 replaces the broader Information concept.

Communication is a stronger fit for the series because it describes an active relationship between parts of a system and leads directly into coordinated mobility.

Infrastructure makes communication physically possible.

Communication allows signals to cross distance.

Mobility then converts coordination into physical movement.

### 18.2 Rationale

Information proved too broad to represent through one concrete image without collapsing into a catalogue of libraries, archives, servers, data, research, and media.

Communication makes a deliberate choice.

Telecommunications becomes the representation channel.

The note does not claim telecommunications is all communication. It uses telecommunications because towers and radiocommunication infrastructure turn an otherwise distributed process into clear visual objects.

### 18.3 Recto: terrestrial communication

The dominant element is a telecommunications mast.

It may stand on a low ridge, at a settlement edge, or in an open technical landscape.

The mast must be tall, structurally credible, and visually isolated.

A small technical shelter may appear as the secondary element.

No visible phones, app interfaces, screens, or floating signal icons are required.

No people are necessary.

The scene says:

signals can cross distance.

### 18.4 Verso: radiocommunications site

The dominant scene is an anonymous radiocommunications site with large satellite dishes.

The imagery may draw from the visual grammar of major European telecommunications centres, but it must not reproduce a named site, landmark, operator, or institution.

Use two or three parabolic dishes at most.

One dish should be clearly dominant.

The others establish scale and networked purpose without creating a catalogue.

A low technical building may appear beneath or behind them.

The open sky is an important compositional field.

The scene should read as long-distance communication before the viewer understands any technical detail.

### 18.5 Conceptual relationship

Recto: terrestrial transmission.

Verso: long-distance radiocommunication.

The pair moves from one vertical mast to broad parabolic forms opening toward the sky.

This gives the €10 two highly distinct silhouettes while preserving one theme.

### 18.6 Colour

Use muted signal red, pale rose, warm red-grey, and near-white.

Most structures should remain within the red family rather than introducing blue sky, green grass, or multi-colour equipment.

Sky and ground may become pale red-grey or near-neutral.

Stronger red is reserved for the numeral and selected structural edges.

### 18.7 Prompt direction

Recto prompt:

Create the recto scene for a horizontal €10 Living Systems euro banknote concept. Communication is represented through telecommunications. Make one tall telecommunications mast the dominant element, isolated against a large quiet sky, with only a small technical shelter or distant terrain as support. Use clean matte architectural visualisation, simplified credible structure, soft daylight, clear perspective, restrained atmosphere, and no realistic people. Keep the palette within a narrow muted-red family, using pale rose, red-grey, and near-white with most form defined by lightness. Do not add screens, smartphones, app icons, floating signal graphics, data streams, logos, dense city detail, or simulated intaglio.

Verso prompt:

Create the verso scene for a horizontal €10 Living Systems euro banknote concept. Show an anonymous large radiocommunications site dominated by one monumental parabolic satellite dish, with one or two secondary dishes and a low technical building. The site should evoke major European telecommunications centres without copying any identifiable facility or branding. Use a broad open sky and strong dish silhouettes. Keep the composition sparse and architectural. Use a clean matte render style and a narrow muted-red colour family modeled mainly through lightness. No crowds, no control-room collage, no data-centre racks, no logos, no institution names, no cyberpunk effects, no glowing signal beams, no fake engraving, and no decorative presentation frame.

## 19. €20: Mobility

### 19.1 Series function

Adaptation

The €20 represents purposeful physical movement.

Rail is the chosen representation channel because it turns movement, coordination, public access, infrastructure, and cross-border connection into a highly legible system.

### 19.2 Rationale

Mobility depends on communication.

Signals, routing, schedules, remote coordination, and changing conditions allow trains to move safely and purposefully rather than blindly.

The note therefore follows naturally from €10.

The previous generic station recto is removed. A station is useful but visually familiar and prone to excessive human activity.

The new recto uses a curved railway bridge as a clear emblem.

### 19.3 Recto: crossing

The dominant element is a sweeping curved rail bridge or viaduct.

The bridge should occupy most of the horizontal composition and create a distinctive arc.

A single passenger train may be the one secondary element.

The surrounding landscape remains simple: water, valley, or open terrain.

Human figures are unnecessary.

The bridge should feel credible, contemporary, and European without reproducing a named structure.

The image says:

mobility crosses space.

### 19.4 Verso: coordination

The dominant subject is a major railway junction viewed from an elevated angle.

The scene is not a giant transport catalogue.

Three or four principal rail paths converge, diverge, and cross through a clearly organised junction.

A signal gantry or switching structure may be the secondary element.

A station, city, freight terminal, and road network should not all compete for attention.

The key image is the geometry of coordinated choice.

This is qualitatively different from the recto bridge.

Recto shows the act of crossing.

Verso shows the system deciding where movement goes.

### 19.5 Conceptual relationship

The transition from €10 to €20 is visible in the signal logic of the junction.

Communication gives mobility coordination.

The €20 therefore represents adaptation not through speed but through the ability to route movement purposefully.

### 19.6 Colour

Use clear rail blue, pale sky-blue, slate-blue, and near-white.

Most landscape and infrastructure remain inside the blue family.

Strong blue is limited to the numeral and selected rail or structural edges.

### 19.7 Prompt direction

Recto prompt:

Create the recto scene for a horizontal €20 Living Systems euro banknote concept. Mobility is represented through rail. Make a sweeping curved railway bridge or viaduct the dominant element, crossing water, a valley, or a restrained open landscape. A single passenger train may appear as the only secondary element. Emphasise the elegant curve and structural silhouette. Use clean matte architectural visualisation, simplified credible engineering, soft daylight, large quiet areas, and no realistic people. Keep the palette within a narrow blue family and model most forms through lightness. Exclude generic station scenes, crowds, vehicle-ad composition, excessive route graphics, futuristic transport, motion blur, logos, simulated intaglio, and decorative framing.

Verso prompt:

Create the verso scene for a horizontal €20 Living Systems euro banknote concept. Show a major railway junction from an elevated oblique viewpoint. Make the branching and converging rail geometry the dominant subject, using only a few principal tracks so the composition remains readable. A signal gantry or switching structure may be secondary. The image should communicate coordination and routing, not transport variety. Use a clean matte architectural-render style, simplified credible rail infrastructure, soft controlled light, and a narrow blue family modeled primarily through lightness. Avoid busy stations, crowds, trams, airports, ports, freight catalogues, glowing maps, branding, fake engraving, and cinematic motion effects.

## 20. €50: Interpretation

### 20.1 Series function

Meaning

The €50 is the conceptual midpoint.

Mobility creates encounters among places, languages, experiences, and points of view.

Interpretation turns those encounters into culture, memory, debate, and shared meaning.

### 20.2 Rationale

Culture is not represented through a catalogue of theatre, museum, cinema, music, literature, translation, workshops, archives, and public debate.

The denomination instead uses two complementary cultural acts.

The recto shows interpretation performed.

The verso shows cultural memory preserved for future reinterpretation.

This keeps the denomination concrete while preserving conceptual depth.

### 20.3 Recto: performance

The dominant element is a contemporary theatre stage seen from the rear or middle of a mostly empty auditorium.

The stage aperture controls the composition.

The performance itself may be abstract and minimal.

A small number of human silhouettes may appear, either on stage or in the audience, but they remain secondary.

Curtains, lighting, and scenery support the aperture rather than compete with it.

The scene should not read as opera luxury, celebrity culture, or historical pageantry.

### 20.4 Verso: cultural memory

The dominant subject is an archival repository or cultural preservation hall.

Use one strong spatial device: a deep central aisle between tall compact shelving, storage systems, or preservation cabinets.

One conservation table or visible stored object may appear as the secondary element.

The archive is not shown as dusty nostalgia.

It is clean, maintained, accessible institutional memory.

This side is intentionally quieter and more inward than the recto.

Recto interpretation is performed in the present.

Verso material is preserved so that interpretation can continue across time.

### 20.5 Conceptual relationship

The pair gives Interpretation two temporal directions:

performance transforms inherited material now;

preservation allows future generations to transform it again.

This also strengthens the bridge toward Environment by making belonging dependent on shared cultural memory rather than on slogans or symbolic icons.

### 20.6 Colour

Use stage amber, terracotta, warm ochre-brown, cream, and light warm grey.

Avoid broad colour jumps.

The theatre and archive should feel like two expressions of the same denomination family.

### 20.7 Prompt direction

Recto prompt:

Create the recto scene for a horizontal €50 Living Systems euro banknote concept. Interpretation is represented through cultural performance. Show a contemporary theatre from the rear or middle of a mostly empty auditorium. Make the stage aperture the dominant element. Use minimal abstract scenery and only a few small silhouettes if needed. Keep curtains, seats, and lights subordinate. Use a clean matte architectural-visualisation style, soft controlled stage light, simplified geometry, and a narrow amber-to-terracotta colour family modeled mostly through lightness. Exclude red-carpet imagery, celebrity portraits, elaborate opera luxury, historical costume spectacle, crowds, realistic faces, fake engraving, generated text, and decorative frames.

Verso prompt:

Create the verso scene for a horizontal €50 Living Systems euro banknote concept. Show a clean archival repository or cultural preservation hall as the dominant subject. Use one deep central aisle between tall compact shelving, preservation cabinets, or organised storage structures, with one small conservation table or stored cultural object as secondary. The architecture should feel maintained and contemporary rather than dusty or nostalgic. Use clean matte architectural visualisation, controlled perspective, sparse human presence or none, and a narrow amber-brown and cream colour family modeled mainly through lightness. Exclude library-reading-room clutter, museum-gallery catalogues, crowds, labels, logos, nostalgic sepia, cinematic mystery, fake intaglio, and decorative outer framing.

## 21. €100: Environment

### 21.1 Series function

Stewardship

The €100 represents the living environmental systems within which every earlier denomination operates.

It is the broadest spatial note and the deliberate exception to the one-object rule.

### 21.2 Rationale

Environment cannot be reduced convincingly to one machine, building, species, or landscape feature.

Its meaning lies in relationships among:

energy;

water;

land;

vegetation;

habitat;

climate;

restoration;

human maintenance.

The €100 is therefore allowed to contain several coordinated elements, but they must belong to one coherent landscape.

This denomination carries the strongest direct Frutiger-Aero character in the series.

Its optimism comes from the visual integration of technology and living systems rather than from decorative bubbles or glossy effects.

### 21.3 Recto: optimistic environmental landscape

The recto is organised around a green open landscape with wind energy as the strongest visual cue.

One large wind turbine may dominate the foreground or near-middle distance, with two or three smaller turbines establishing rhythm.

Water, planted terrain, wetland, or a restored river may occupy the lower field.

The landscape may include a modest renewable or monitoring element, but the composition must remain calm.

People are unnecessary.

The note should feel airy, lucid, optimistic, and unmistakably environmental.

### 21.4 Verso: integrated stewardship system

The verso shows a broader environmental landscape in which several systems coexist.

Possible elements include:

wind turbines;

restored wetland or river;

reforested areas;

managed agricultural land;

solar surfaces;

water-management structures;

ecological monitoring.

These are not presented as equal icons.

The dominant subject is the continuous landscape and the relationships across it.

One river or contour flow should organise the composition.

Wind turbines may remain the strongest repeated technical form.

The scene should show stewardship rather than untouched wilderness.

### 21.5 Conceptual relationship

The recto offers the optimistic image of environmental integration.

The verso explains that environmental quality depends on coordinated systems rather than on one isolated technology.

This is why the €100 is intentionally more plural than the other denominations.

### 21.6 Colour

Use fresh green, jade, pale green-cyan, moss-grey, and near-white.

The palette may have slightly more internal variation than the other notes, but it should still remain within a narrow neighbouring hue family.

Blue water should normally be translated toward green-cyan rather than becoming a separate blue object.

### 21.7 Prompt direction

Recto prompt:

Create the recto scene for a horizontal €100 Living Systems euro banknote concept. Environment is represented through an integrated optimistic landscape. Make one large wind turbine the strongest visual cue, with two or three smaller turbines forming a restrained rhythm across green terrain. Include calm water, wetland, or a restored river as a secondary landscape element. The scene should be airy, bright, and clean, with Frutiger-Aero optimism expressed through the coexistence of technology, vegetation, water, and open sky. Use a matte architectural-landscape render style, simplified forms, no realistic people, and a narrow green-to-green-cyan palette modeled mainly through lightness. Avoid eco-advertising, giant logos, saturated rainbow colour, untouched-wilderness romanticism, excessive object counts, fake engraving, and decorative framing.

Verso prompt:

Create the verso scene for a horizontal €100 Living Systems euro banknote concept. Show one continuous environmental stewardship landscape containing several coordinated systems: wind turbines, restored wetland or river, reforested areas, managed agricultural land, limited solar surfaces, water-management structures, and discreet monitoring infrastructure. The landscape itself is the dominant subject. Use one river, valley, or contour flow to organise the whole composition so it does not become a collage. Wind turbines may provide the strongest repeated technical silhouette. Use a clean matte architectural-landscape visualisation, restrained atmosphere, no realistic crowds, and a narrow green family with most differentiation through lightness. Exclude equal-weight icon catalogues, corporate sustainability branding, untouched wilderness, disaster imagery, excessive saturation, fake intaglio, and decorative outer frames.

## 22. €200: Future

### 22.1 Series function

Possibility

The €200 closes the sequence with ambition rather than certainty.

Future is broader than space exploration, but space is selected as the representation channel because it is immediate, intuitive, and capable of expressing exploration, perspective, distance, technical ambition, and the unknown.

The scene does not predict that Europe's future is “space.”

It uses space exploration as a readable image of future-facing collective capability.

### 22.2 Rationale

The previous observatory and generic research-landscape concept was too diffuse.

It contained too many possible futures at once and left the image model without a dominant subject.

The revised note makes a choice.

Recto: the dream and perspective of exploration.

Verso: the terrestrial system that makes the dream technically possible.

### 22.3 Recto: orbital circularisation

The dominant element is an anonymous European-style orbital launcher or upper stage during circularisation.

The vehicle crosses the composition diagonally or laterally.

Earth is the only major secondary element.

The planet is simplified, large, and partially cut by the right boundary of the banknote.

The composition should create a sense of perspective and distance rather than a heroic action scene.

Any engine plume is restrained and technically plausible.

No astronaut is required.

No logo, programme name, flag, or recognisable institutional livery appears on the vehicle.

The image should feel like exploration, perspective, and dream.

It must not become space opera.

### 22.4 Verso: terrestrial capability

The dominant subject is an anonymous space centre.

The scene is built around one large vehicle-assembly or integration building.

A launch tower or pad may be the one secondary dominant structure.

A simple service route connects them.

Additional laboratories, offices, tracking systems, storage, and technical buildings remain small and subordinate.

The goal is not to catalogue every function of a space programme.

The image communicates that exploration depends on large, maintained terrestrial systems.

The site remains anonymous and temporally durable.

No ESA branding.

No launch-company branding.

No national flags.

No identifiable real facility.

### 22.5 Conceptual relationship

Recto: what collective capability reaches toward.

Verso: what collective capability requires on the ground.

The contrast is intentionally strong.

The recto is sparse, orbital, and almost abstract.

The verso is terrestrial, architectural, and infrastructural.

The pair therefore avoids the previous problem of the verso being the recto with more detail.

### 22.6 Colour

Use solar ochre, warm yellow, cream, pale amber, and warm grey.

Earth should be translated into the denomination family rather than rendered as a fully blue-and-green globe.

Space does not need to be black.

A warm pale or deep ochre-grey field may preserve the denomination palette while still reading as orbital space.

The palette must avoid metallic wealth coding.

### 22.7 Prompt direction

Recto prompt:

Create the recto scene for a horizontal €200 Living Systems euro banknote concept. Future is represented through space exploration. Make an anonymous European-style orbital launcher or upper stage during circularisation the dominant element. Place a simplified Earth behind it as the only major secondary element, with the planet partially cut by the right boundary of the banknote. Create a strong sense of perspective, distance, exploration, and open possibility. Use a clean matte visualisation style, simplified credible aerospace geometry, restrained engine plume if any, and a narrow solar-ochre, warm-yellow, cream, and amber-grey palette modeled mainly through lightness. No logos, no ESA branding, no programme names, no national flags, no astronauts, no cinematic starfield, no space-opera effects, no fake engraving, and no decorative outer frame.

Verso prompt:

Create the verso scene for a horizontal €200 Living Systems euro banknote concept. Show an anonymous terrestrial space centre as the system that makes exploration possible. Make one large vehicle-assembly or integration building the dominant element and one launch tower or pad the secondary element, connected by a simple service route. Keep laboratories, tracking equipment, storage, and technical buildings small and subordinate. Use a clean matte architectural-render style, simplified credible engineering, sparse human presence or none, and a narrow solar-ochre and warm-yellow-brown palette modeled primarily through lightness. The facility must not resemble one exact real site. No ESA branding, no company logos, no flags, no institution names, no dense futuristic city, no heroic launch spectacle, no fake intaglio, and no decorative framing.

## 24. Continuity across denominations

Cross-denomination continuity is carried primarily by the dependency narrative, not by hidden object scavenger hunts.

Optional close-reading links may appear, but they are never required for comprehension.

Examples:

the €5 power grid supports the €10 communications infrastructure;

the €10 communication system supports the signal logic visible in the €20 rail junction;

the €20's movement supports the cultural encounter represented by the €50;

the €50's preservation of cultural memory supports the belonging that motivates the €100's stewardship;

the €100 preserves environmental conditions inherited by the €200 future;

the €200 space centre visibly depends on electricity, communication, mobility, interpretation, and environmental management, returning the sequence to its foundations.

These relationships may be implied structurally rather than through literal cameo objects.

## 25. Security-ready visual opportunities

Security-ready opportunities remain subordinate to the approved visual concept.

Possible pattern families include:

€5 transmission-line rhythms and grid intersections;

€10 radio-wave arcs and antenna structures;

€20 rail points and parallel route curves;

€50 stage arcs, page edges, and archive rhythms;

€100 contour lines, wind patterns, leaf-water branching;

€200 orbital arcs and trajectory geometry.

The Europe map may support layered or optically variable treatment while preserving one stable silhouette.

Tactile rhythms can derive from denomination-specific border logic.

Watermarks may use simplified dominant silhouettes rather than miniature scenes.

These are visual opportunities, not tested security devices.

## 26. Recognition and public comprehension

The first reading is direct.

€5: electricity infrastructure.

€10: telecommunications.

€20: rail mobility.

€50: cultural interpretation and memory.

€100: environmental stewardship.

€200: future-facing exploration.

The viewer does not need to know the precise engineering function of a telecommunications dish, railway junction, or launch facility to understand the broader category.

Large numeral, colour, dominant silhouette, spatial type, border rhythm, and physical proportion work together.

## 29. Evaluation summary

### 29.1 Visual harmony and coherence

All six notes share:

a horizontal monetary structure;

matte architectural visualisation;

restricted human presence;

denomination-led hue families;

stable institutional elements;

strong silhouette hierarchy;

controlled quiet space;

consistent crop discipline.

Coherence comes from common design grammar rather than identical scene construction.

### 29.2 Thematic consistency

Every denomination names a broad system and selects one concrete representation channel.

The recto is emblematic.

The verso is systemic.

The six roles form one functional sequence from physical substrate to open future.

### 29.3 Visual appeal and aesthetic quality

The revision prioritises clear dominant forms over scene quantity.

Each side should remain visually understandable before close details are inspected.

The €100 is intentionally richer, but its complexity is organised by one continuous landscape.

### 29.4 Symbolism and cultural representation of the EU

The series represents Europe through shared capability:

electricity infrastructure;

telecommunications;

rail mobility;

culture and memory;

environmental stewardship;

future-facing research and exploration.

The imagery remains anonymous enough to outlive specific operators and institutions.

### 29.5 Denomination discrimination

Independent signals include:

physical proportion;

colour family;

dominant recto silhouette;

dominant verso silhouette;

border rhythm;

tonal distribution;

tactile group;

spatial type.

### 29.6 Public recognition

The revised scenes use concrete objects rather than abstract institutional composites.

Transmission towers, satellite dishes, railway bridges, stages, wind turbines, launch vehicles, and large technical facilities are easier to recognise than generic “campuses” or mixed-function complexes.

### 29.7 Accessibility

Large numerals, strong local contrast, tactile reservations, denomination-specific silhouettes, grayscale separation, and simplified scene hierarchy support accessibility.

Final accessibility performance requires specialist testing.

### 29.8 Creativity and originality

The proposal translates the optimistic worldview associated with Frutiger Aero into a restrained monetary and architectural language.

Its originality lies less in surface decoration than in using clean technological and environmental imagery to construct a dependency narrative across six denominations.

## 30. Closing statement

Living Systems begins with the physical substrate of modern life and ends with an object leaving Earth.

Between those points, electricity enables communication, communication enables coordinated movement, movement broadens interpretation, interpretation produces belonging, belonging motivates stewardship, and stewardship preserves the ability to attempt what does not yet exist.

The series does not try to show everything each concept can mean.

It makes choices.

One denomination, one broad system, one concrete representation channel.

Most scenes have one dominant element.

The €100 alone becomes deliberately plural because environment is itself a network of relationships.

The result is intended to feel optimistic without becoming nostalgic, technological without becoming corporate, environmental without becoming promotional, and futuristic without becoming speculative spectacle.

The notes present public capability as something built, maintained, shared, and inherited.

## Why the revision was necessary

Version 1 defined the conceptual taxonomy more successfully than it defined the images.

Its main visual failure was not lack of detail but lack of hierarchy. Several scenes attempted to represent all aspects of a motif simultaneously, producing generic campuses, mixed institutions, transport catalogues, and research landscapes without a clear dominant subject.

Version 2 converts each abstract category into a chosen visual channel and gives each side a concrete compositional anchor.

The revision therefore moves the proposal from “illustrate the full definition of the system” to “choose one image that makes the system immediately legible.”

That principle is now the basis for all further design generation.
