## 1. Executive summary

Living Systems presents Europe through six interdependent capacities that make collective life possible.

The series is deliberately not encyclopaedic. Each denomination names a broad system and resolves it through concrete visual manifestations. Infrastructure is represented through electricity. Communication is represented through telecommunications. Mobility is represented through urban transit and port logistics. Interpretation is represented through higher education and orchestral music. Environment is represented through a public garden and a managed watershed. Future is represented through space exploration.

A representation channel does not exhaust the meaning of a motif. The purpose of the choice is visual clarity. Each scene must have a principal noun, a resolved viewpoint, and a stable hierarchy before it carries secondary meaning.

The six denominations form a dependency sequence rather than a hierarchy of importance.

Infrastructure enables communication because communication requires reliable energy, physical networks, buildings, maintenance, and shared standards.

Communication enables mobility because purposeful movement depends on signalling, coordination, routing, schedules, changing conditions, and the exchange of information across distance.

Mobility broadens interpretation because the movement of people and goods creates contact, access, exchange, and new points of view.

Interpretation creates belonging because education, judgment, art, and shared cultural forms give places, histories, and communities meaning.

Belonging supports environmental stewardship because people are more likely to care for places they understand as meaningful and inherited.

Environmental stewardship preserves the future because ecological health and maintained public systems keep options open for generations that have not yet arrived.

The recto and verso have different narrative jobs.

The recto is emblematic. It presents a direct encounter with the denomination's system: transmission infrastructure, a self-supporting lattice mobile tower, urban mobility at street level, a university lecture, a public garden, or orbital exploration.

The verso reveals the broader apparatus, institution, or system that exists beyond the first experience. It can use a larger complexity budget, but that complexity remains organised around a resolved centre of gravity. The reverse must reveal a qualitatively different layer of the system rather than repeat the recto with more objects.

Visual hierarchy is a governing rule. Most scenes contain one dominant organising subject and tightly subordinated supporting elements. The €100 verso is the deliberate landscape exception because Environment is most legible as an interacting system.

Human figures remain sparse and anonymous. They appear only where their presence is necessary to make an experience legible, especially the €20 urban scene and the €50 lecture. No realistic face or individual protagonist is required.

The image language is neither photorealistic nor cartoon-like. It is derived from clean architectural visualisation: matte surfaces, simplified geometry, controlled perspective, restrained atmospheric depth, soft lighting, and clear silhouettes. Frutiger Aero is inherited conceptually through optimism, environmental integration, clean technology, open atmosphere, and legible systems rather than through literal desktop imagery, skeuomorphism, glossy interface effects, or nostalgic imitation.

Exterior scenes share a temperate or boreal European environmental base. Ground, vegetation, water, and planting use regionally plausible meadows, grasses, deciduous and coniferous trees, hedgerows, reeds, wetlands, civic planting, and cool-climate soils. Technical sites are integrated into maintained terrain through roads, paving, drainage, water systems, planting, and service surfaces. The series does not use arid scrub, desert ground, red-rock terrain, cacti, yucca, or Mojave-like bushland.

Architecture is functional and structurally legible. Visual richness comes from massing, engineering, materials, circulation, and landscape integration. Bas-reliefs, statues, sculpted façade panels, pseudo-classical trim, fantasy supports, and unrelated prestige ornament do not belong to the series.

Colour is denomination-led and hue-uniform. Each note has one dominant colour family. Most modelling occurs through lightness and density rather than through changes of hue. Except for required institutional colours and near-neutral paper tones, the named denomination palette remains the executable colour rule and no independent secondary hue family is introduced. An approximately twelve-percent arc of the colour wheel around the denomination hue is retained as a qualitative benchmark for how narrow that family should feel, not as a mathematical production tolerance or an image-generation parameter.

The governing requirement is:

> One clear image first; system meaning through structure; detail only after hierarchy is secure.

## 2. Theme, narrative, and motifs

### 2.1 Theme

The theme concerns the systems through which Europeans live together.

Infrastructure, communication, mobility, interpretation, environment, and future are not isolated policy areas. They are connected civic capacities. Each relies on systems that are distributed, maintained, and usually taken for granted when they work well.

Money is an appropriate carrier for this theme because currency is itself a shared system. A banknote exists through standards, recognition, circulation, trust, maintenance, and institutional continuity.

The European dimension lies in networks that cross borders without requiring a catalogue of national symbols. Electricity grids, telecommunications, railways, culture, ecosystems, and scientific ambitions operate locally while depending on wider cooperation.

The central tension is optimism and responsibility.

The proposal is optimistic in its spatial language: open skies, clean technical forms, maintained landscapes, legible infrastructure, and technology integrated with public life. That optimism is not automatic. Versoes reveal the material systems that make it possible.

### 2.2 Narrative

Across the series:

Infrastructure creates a reliable substrate.

Communication allows signals to travel across that substrate.

Mobility turns communication and coordination into purposeful physical movement.

Interpretation turns movement and encounter into meaning.

Environment turns belonging into stewardship.

Future extends stewardship into responsibility for possibilities not yet fixed.

The €50 marks the main conceptual change of register. The first three denominations concern physical substrate, signal exchange, and movement. The €50 asks what those capacities produce culturally.

The €100 then expands the object of care from human institutions to the landscapes and ecosystems within which they exist.

The €200 closes the sequence with aspiration rather than prediction.

Its question is:

> What can maintained systems allow future generations to attempt?

### 2.3 Representation channels

A broad motif is not an instruction to place every associated object in one scene.

Each denomination therefore resolves its broad concept through concrete manifestations selected for public legibility.

€5, Infrastructure

Infrastructure is represented through electricity. The recto shows transmission as the visible substrate of modern life. The verso reveals generation through an anonymous nuclear reactor-building complex whose cooling towers remain clearly visible as part of the system.

€10, Communication

Communication is represented through telecommunications. The recto shows one self-supporting lattice mobile tower from ground level at a low angle. The verso shifts to a high elevated view over one coherent radiocommunications complex: one dominant parabolic dish and exactly two smaller dishes aligned along the same technical site.

€20, Mobility

Mobility is represented through two forms of circulation. The recto shows the everyday movement of people through a tram intersection and station in a contemporary European city. The verso reveals the less visible circulation of goods through a major seaport organised around one container ship, a row of cranes, and onward rail and road connections.

€50, Interpretation

Interpretation is represented through two complementary manifestations. The recto shows higher education as an active process of examining and transmitting knowledge through a university lecture. The verso shows cultural form materialised institutionally through an unoccupied orchestral stage already set inside a concert hall.

€100, Environment

Environment is represented through two scales of stewardship. The recto shows the experience of environment through a contemporary public garden organised around water and planting. The verso shifts to a high landscape view of the broader watershed system that sustains environmental quality, integrating renewable energy, water management, restoration, forestry, and agriculture.

€200, Future

Future is represented through space exploration. The recto shows an anonymous upper stage in a near-horizontal prograde orbital attitude with Earth behind it. The verso shifts inside an anonymous vehicle-integration hall where rocket stages and engine components are assembled through cranes, platforms, and technical infrastructure.

### 2.4 Why these motifs

The motifs are selected for immediate visual legibility and for qualitative difference between the two sides of each denomination.

Transmission towers, reactor buildings and cooling towers, a self-supporting lattice mobile tower, aligned satellite dishes, a tram intersection, a container port, a university lecture hall, an unoccupied orchestral stage, a public garden, a managed watershed, an orbital upper stage, and a rocket integration hall all provide clear spatial identities.

The viewer does not need to decode a diagram or read explanatory text before understanding the principal image. Interpretation can deepen afterward.

The series therefore prefers a resolved concrete scene over a comprehensive but weak illustration of a category.

## 3. Assessment framework

Living Systems is structured against the published euro-banknote design framework.

The proposal retains the need for:

coherence across the €5, €10, €20, €50, €100, and €200;

clear distinction between denominations;

accessibility;

technical feasibility;

common European institutional elements;

consistency between theme and imagery;

provision for the European flag;

a stable map of Europe;

a signature area;

tactile identification;

later integration of security features.

The published visual and technical criteria are:

| Criterion | Published weighting |
| --------- | -------------------: |
| Visual harmony and coherence of the complete series | 10% |
| Thematic consistency with the story | 10% |
| Visual appeal and aesthetic quality | 20% |
| Symbolism and cultural representation of the EU | 10% |
| Discrimination between denominations | 10% |
| Public recognition | 5% |
| Accessibility | 10% |
| Creativity and originality | 25% |

The common system consists of:

a shared recto and verso grid;

a common humanist typographic system;

stable institutional fields;

narrow denomination-led colour families;

matte architectural visualisation;

consistent scene hierarchy;

a stable relationship between dominant motif, open space, and institutional information;

a common temperate or boreal European environmental base for exterior scenes;

a common site-integration rule for technical buildings and infrastructure;

a common rule of functional, unornamented structural design;

a common rule that the scene ends at the note boundary.

The principal variables are:

denomination proportion;

dominant hue;

recto subject;

verso subject;

silhouette;

border rhythm;

light-dark distribution;

tactile group.

Accessibility is supported through dimensions, numerals, contrast, silhouette, border pattern, tactile reservations, and scene type, not colour alone.

Security features are not presented as finished technical solutions. Only security-ready zones and transformable pattern opportunities are identified.

## 4. Conceptual basis

### 4.1 A living system as a design subject

The title Living Systems describes a model of public life rather than a biological theme.

A living system is connected, maintained, adaptive, and dependent on flows.

Electricity flows through grids.

Signals move through communication networks.

People and goods move through transport networks.

Interpretation moves through education, scholarship, artistic practice, performance, and cultural institutions.

Water, energy, species, and human activity interact across landscapes.

Future capability depends on institutions that preserve knowledge, maintain infrastructure, and support long-term ambition.

The series therefore treats Europe as a set of relationships rather than a set of isolated monuments.

### 4.2 Frutiger Aero as conceptual inheritance

The proposal takes Frutiger Aero seriously as an optimistic worldview, not as a checklist of visual effects.

The relevant inheritance is:

technology integrated with everyday life;

clean public infrastructure;

open skies and atmospheric light;

water and vegetation as active parts of designed environments;

rounded but functional geometry;

legible humanist information design;

confidence that technological and environmental systems can coexist;

a bright, civic, forward-looking mood.

The proposal does not require:

skeuomorphism;

glossy glass buttons;

floating bubbles;

desktop wallpaper compositions;

operating-system references;

consumer electronics;

lens flare;

decorative chrome;

interface imitation.

Frutiger Aero is therefore conceptual rather than literal. Its optimism survives, while the imagery is translated into a disciplined banknote system.

### 4.3 Comprehension without prior knowledge

A viewer should be able to read each note without knowing the term Frutiger Aero.

€5: electricity is transmitted and generated.

€10: signals are transmitted across distance.

€20: people move through a city while goods move through a port and logistics network.

€50: knowledge is interpreted through higher education, while cultural interpretation takes durable form through orchestral institutions.

€100: a cared-for garden belongs to a wider environmental system of water, land, energy, and restoration.

€200: orbital exploration depends on terrestrial engineering and assembly capability.

The visual reference adds atmosphere, not meaning required for comprehension.

### 4.4 European scope and anonymity

The designs avoid named institutions and specific national branding.

Real sites, technologies, landscapes, and architectural precedents inform the imagery, while the final scenes remain anonymous and non-identifiable.

Exterior environments use temperate and boreal European cues rather than generic desert scrubland. Urban scenes use plausible contemporary European street geometry and planting without reproducing one city.

A nuclear power station should not reproduce one identifiable plant.

The €10 radiocommunications site takes visual inspiration from European satellite-communication facilities without reproducing a protected landmark, company identity, national operator, logo, or exact site plan.

The €20 port uses the visual grammar of major European seaports without copying one harbour plan, shipping company, terminal operator, or city skyline.

The €50 lecture hall and concert hall remain anonymous institutional spaces rather than reproductions of named universities, orchestras, or venues.

The €200 launcher and integration hall recall the proportions and engineering logic of European launch systems without carrying ESA, Ariane, company, national, or programme branding.

This anonymity follows a general monetary principle: the scene should remain meaningful even as real institutions, operators, technologies, and organisations evolve.

## 5. Visual reference and monetary precedent

### 5.1 Primary visual reference

The visual reference is early twenty-first-century architectural and civic visualisation interpreted through the optimistic logic associated with Frutiger Aero.

Useful characteristics include:

clean matte surfaces;

simple geometric massing;

soft environmental light;

clear air and water;

legible infrastructure;

controlled rounded forms;

open horizons;

environmental integration;

technical objects treated as civic rather than industrial spectacle.

### 5.2 Monetary translation

The scene should feel integrated into the banknote rather than pasted on top of it.

This does not mean imitating intaglio engraving.

Narrative scenes use tonal, architectural-render treatment with limited texture and simplified material behaviour. Banknote structure is provided by composition, typography, borders, numerals, institutional fields, subtle line patterns, and tonal integration.

The design avoids the assumption that monetary seriousness requires simulated nineteenth-century engraving.

### 5.3 What is not transferred

The proposal excludes:

literal software aesthetics;

skeuomorphic controls;

photoreal commercial rendering;

cartoon rendering;

painterly fantasy;

cinematic visual effects;

glossy product visualisation;

highly saturated eco-branding;

ornamental pseudo-security linework inside the narrative scene;

dense intaglio imitation.

## 6. Narrative structure

### 6.1 Recto role

The recto is emblematic.

It presents one concrete manifestation of the denomination's system at the scale of encounter.

The recto should normally contain:

one dominant organising subject;

tightly controlled secondary content;

a restrained environment;

large areas of visual rest.

The image must remain recognisable at thumbnail scale.

### 6.2 Verso role

The verso reveals what lies beyond the first experience.

It answers:

What broader apparatus, institution, supply system, production system, or landscape exists behind the recto?

What part of modern life normally remains invisible from the immediate encounter?

What wider capacity does that hidden system sustain?

The verso can use more detail than the recto, but its detail must belong to one resolved spatial system.

Across the series, the reverse normally changes scale, camera position, or manifestation. Most versos reveal a broader apparatus or system that makes the recto experience possible. The €20 and €50 are deliberate exceptions: their versos use complementary manifestations because port logistics and orchestral culture broaden Mobility and Interpretation more effectively than a literal enabling apparatus would.

The resolved pairs are:

transmission towers become a nuclear reactor-building complex with cooling towers and grid connection;

a ground-level self-supporting lattice mobile tower becomes a high-angle radiocommunications complex whose three dishes form one coherent site;

a ground-level tram intersection becomes a high-angle seaport logistics system;

a university lecture becomes an unoccupied orchestral stage setup inside a concert hall;

a public garden becomes a managed watershed landscape;

a near-horizontal orbital upper stage becomes an interior rocket integration and assembly hall.

### 6.3 Series progression

€5 provides physical substrate.

€10 allows communication across distance.

€20 converts coordination into the circulation of people and goods.

€50 converts encounter and knowledge into interpretation, judgment, and cultural form.

€100 converts belonging into stewardship.

€200 converts stewardship into preserved possibility and ambition.

## 7. Scene hierarchy

### 7.1 Dominant-element rule

Every scene begins with one visual decision:

What is the dominant element?

If that answer cannot be stated in a short noun phrase, the scene is not ready.

The dominant element controls:

largest occupied area;

strongest silhouette;

highest local contrast;

principal directional rhythm;

main overlap;

first thumbnail reading.

Secondary elements exist only to clarify scale, context, and system relationship.

### 7.2 Maximum scene complexity

Most rectos contain:

one dominant element;

at most one named secondary element;

a restrained supporting environment;

minimal fine detail.

Versos have a larger complexity budget because their role is systemic. They can contain several subordinate technical components when those components belong to one resolved system. They still require one unmistakable centre of gravity.

No scene contains several equally important buildings, vehicles, devices, human activities, diagrams, and landscapes.

The image model must never have to guess what matters most.

### 7.3 Deliberate exception: €100

Environment is the one denomination designed around a coordinated living landscape rather than a single technical object.

This is not permission for a collage.

The recto remains simple: one public garden organised around a pond and one footbridge.

The verso is deliberately richer: one watershed landscape organised by a single river, with four wind turbines on one ridge, one compact solar field, restored wetland, reforestation, managed agriculture, and one low water-control structure.

Every element belongs to the same spatial system.

The €100 is therefore the most visually expansive and the most directly Frutiger-Aero denomination in the series.

### 7.4 Quiet space

Open sky, calm water, pale terrain, matte walls, and simple tonal fields are active compositional elements.

Empty space is not unfinished space.

It protects hierarchy and makes the dominant object readable.

### 7.5 Resolved scene specification

The proposal is a decision document, not an exploration document.

Every final scene description fixes:

the dominant subject;

the number and identity of secondary elements;

the camera height and viewing angle;

the spatial setting;

the main directional geometry;

the human-presence rule;

the colour family;

the lighting and rendering language;

the major exclusions.

Words that introduce alternatives, including “or”, “may”, “could”, “perhaps”, “either”, “possibly”, “around”, “such as”, and “preferably”, require review when they affect visible content. They remain valid in conceptual explanation, comparison, exclusion, and non-visual technical discussion. They are removed from final scene specifications when they create a choice that should already have been resolved.

A final scene description must not ask the image model to choose its protagonist, setting, viewpoint, secondary structure, or scene type.

## 8. Visual rendering style

### 8.1 Target style

The narrative imagery uses a clean architectural-render visual language.

The target is:

deliberately simplified;

matte;

spatially coherent;

clean;

calm;

readable;

idealised but credible.

It should resemble a carefully art-directed architectural visualisation rather than photography, cartoon illustration, or painterly rendering.

### 8.2 Geometry

Forms are simplified without becoming toy-like.

Complex engineering structures retain enough detail to be credible, but small components are grouped into larger readable masses.

Edges are clean.

Perspective is controlled.

Surfaces do not require microscopic texture.

### 8.3 Materials

Materials are communicated primarily through lightness, edge behaviour, and restrained reflectivity.

Concrete is matte.

Metal is satin rather than mirror-like.

Glass is lightly translucent without interface-like glow.

Vegetation is simplified into mass and silhouette rather than individually rendered leaves.

Water is clean and calm unless movement is compositionally necessary.

### 8.4 European climate and surface

Every exterior scene uses a temperate or boreal European environmental base appropriate to its function.

Permitted ground and vegetation vocabulary includes:

managed meadow;

cool-climate grass;

low perennial planting;

deciduous trees;

conifers;

hedgerows;

reeds and wetland vegetation;

riverbank planting;

temperate civic trees;

damp or neutral-toned soil;

gravel, ballast, paving, service concrete, and maintained technical surfaces.

Exterior scenes do not use arid scrub, desert ground, red-rock terrain, cacti, yucca, dry desert bushes, or Mojave-like vegetation.

The exact amount of vegetation changes by scene, but the climate family remains coherent across the series.

### 8.5 Site integration

Major structures are integrated into designed sites.

Technical buildings connect visibly to access roads, service surfaces, drainage, water infrastructure, paving, planting, rail, quays, or utility routes appropriate to the scene.

Landscape transitions meet structures deliberately through grading, retaining edges, paths, embankments, wetland margins, or maintained ground.

Buildings do not sit as isolated objects on undefined empty terrain.

### 8.6 Structural restraint

Architecture derives visual interest from function, proportion, massing, structure, circulation, and material.

Façades remain clean and unornamented.

The series excludes bas-reliefs, heroic statuary, sculpted façade panels, pseudo-classical trim, fantasy buttresses, decorative arches with no structural role, monumental plaques, and unrelated prestige ornament.

Technical structures remain credible to their function.

### 8.7 Lighting

Lighting is soft and directional.

Scenes avoid dramatic cinematic contrast.

The mood is bright, lucid, and slightly idealised.

Shadows clarify form but do not dominate.

Highlights remain restrained.

### 8.8 Human presence

Realistic people are avoided.

When people are necessary to establish lived experience, they appear as small simplified anonymous silhouettes.

No face is required.

No individual figure carries portrait-like narrative weight.

No crowd becomes a visual texture.

### 8.9 Relation to print

Scenes should feel embedded into the banknote through restricted colour, tonal integration, cropped composition, and common graphic structure.

They should not be converted into fake engraving.

Fine geometric border and security-ready patterns sit outside and around the narrative scene, while the principal image remains a matte architectural visualisation.

## 9. Monetary design principles

### 9.1 Visual authority

The notes must read as money before the theme is analysed.

Each design integrates:

a large denomination numeral;

EURO, ΕΥΡΩ, and ЕВРО;

the European flag;

a stable Europe map;

a signature field;

serial areas;

tactile reservations;

security-ready zones;

a controlled outer frame;

a denomination-specific pattern family;

a strong tonal hierarchy.

The monetary scaffold and narrative scene are composed together.

### 9.2 Two reading distances

From a distance, the viewer reads:

denomination;

physical proportion;

colour family;

dominant silhouette;

large light-dark pattern.

At close range, the viewer discovers:

technical structure;

small environmental relationships;

maintenance access;

signal and route logic;

border patterns;

map treatment;

secondary continuity cues.

### 9.3 Detail policy

Detail is permitted only after the dominant form is secure.

Close-reading detail must not create new focal points.

The proposal therefore rejects “detail as quantity.” Detail is valuable when it explains structure, material, maintenance, or use.

### 9.4 Horizontal use

Principal elements remain legible when the note is:

held horizontally;

partially covered;

stacked;

fanned;

viewed quickly during payment.

Large numerals remain visible near the short edges.

## 10. Colour policy

### 10.1 General rule

Each denomination is hue-uniform.

The note uses one dominant chromatic family with reasonable saturation.

Most visual differentiation occurs through:

lightness;

shadow;

density;

transparency;

near-neutral paper tones.

Except for required institutional colours and neutrals, the executable requirement is to remain inside the named denomination palette without introducing an independent secondary hue family.

An approximately twelve-percent arc of the colour wheel around the denomination hue is retained as a qualitative benchmark for the intended narrowness of the family. It is explanatory guidance, not a mathematical production tolerance and not a parameter to be executed literally by the image model.

### 10.2 Why hue uniformity matters

A narrow hue family:

strengthens denomination recognition;

reduces competition inside the scene;

helps complex structures read as one printed object;

prevents the scene from becoming a colourful illustration pasted onto currency;

makes lightness carry hierarchy;

creates stronger family resemblance across recto and verso.

### 10.3 Denomination palettes

| Denomination | Dominant family | Main interpretation |
| ------------ | --------------- | ------------------- |
| €5 | Grey | cool pearl grey, graphite, concrete, silver-grey |
| €10 | Red | muted signal red, pale rose, warm red-grey |
| €20 | Blue | clear mobility blue, pale sky-blue, slate-blue |
| €50 | Orange-brown | amber, terracotta, warm ochre-brown, cream |
| €100 | Green | fresh green, jade, pale green-cyan, moss-grey |
| €200 | Yellow-brown | solar ochre, warm yellow, cream, pale amber |

The €5 is predominantly achromatic with a small cool bias and no broad hue range.

### 10.4 Saturation

Saturation remains moderate.

The brightest chroma is reserved for:

principal numeral;

small route and signal accents;

selected structural edges;

specific environmental cues.

Large surfaces should not glow.

### 10.5 Grayscale performance

Each denomination remains distinguishable without colour.

€5: transmission-tower verticals, reactor domes, and cooling-tower silhouettes.

€10: singular self-supporting lattice mobile-tower vertical and broad aligned dish geometry.

€20: street-level tram-track intersection and high-angle ship-and-crane port mass.

€50: stepped lecture-hall geometry and semicircular orchestra arrangement.

€100: garden water curve and watershed contour structure.

€200: near-horizontal orbital silhouette and large interior integration-hall geometry.

## 11. Series-wide composition

### 11.1 Format and proportion

The six notes retain denomination-specific horizontal euro proportions.

| Denomination | Approximate working ratio |
| ------------ | ------------------------: |
| €5 | 1.94:1 |
| €10 | 1.90:1 |
| €20 | 1.85:1 |
| €50 | 1.82:1 |
| €100 | 1.91:1 |
| €200 | 1.99:1 |

A 1.9:1 master frame is used during early composition.

The image ends at the note edge.

Presentation margins, photographed-paper backgrounds, rotation, perspective skew, and drop shadows are excluded from the design plate.

### 11.2 Recto composition and canonical mask

Layout coordinates are normalised to the note frame: x runs from 0 at the left edge to 100 at the right edge; y runs from 0 at the top edge to 100 at the bottom edge.

The canonical recto layout is named `R`.

| Mask | Normalised bounds | Function |
| ---- | ----------------- | -------- |
| `R-IMG` | x 0–78, y 0–100 | high-detail narrative image field; dominant subject and dense secondary structure remain inside this field |
| `R-QUIET` | x 78–100, y 0–100 | low-detail right-edge quiet rail supporting monetary information |
| `R-VALUE` | x 80–100, y 6–44 | principal numeral field |
| `R-INST` | x 72–100, y 48–94 | institutional group mask containing type, flag, signature reservation, and serial reservation |
| `R-TYPE` | x 72–98, y 48–60 | EURO / ΕΥΡΩ / ЕВРО field |
| `R-FLAG` | x 86–97, y 64–74 | European flag field |
| `R-SIGN` | x 74–97, y 77–84 | signature reservation for later controlled artwork |
| `R-SERIAL` | x 68–97, y 88–94 | serial reservation for later controlled artwork |
| `R-TACT` | x 94–100, y 84–100 | tactile reservation |

The narrative image remains full bleed to the note edge, but only low-detail, low-contrast continuation or a large low-frequency background form may extend from `R-IMG` into `R-QUIET` beneath the protected right-edge fields. The €200 Earth limb is the defined example of this permitted background overlap. The dominant subject and high-frequency detail do not enter `R-VALUE`, `R-TYPE`, `R-FLAG`, `R-SIGN`, `R-SERIAL`, or `R-TACT`.

| ---- | ----------------- | -------- |

| `V-VALUE` | x 0–18, y 6–44 | principal numeral field |

The systemic image remains full bleed to the note edge, but only low-detail, low-contrast continuation may extend from `V-IMG` beneath the protected left-edge fields. The dominant subject and high-frequency detail do not enter `V-VALUE`, `V-TYPE`, `V-MAP`, `V-SERIAL`, or `V-TACT`.

The verso image composition differs substantially from the recto whenever that difference is required to reveal the broader system.

Series coherence comes from typography, value placement, colour, borders, map treatment, and crop logic rather than from forcing the same scenic arrangement on both sides.

### 11.4 Shared alignments and orientation rule

The following remain stable:

principal numeral masks;

EURO / ΕΥΡΩ / ЕВРО masks and hierarchy;

flag location on the recto;

map field on the verso;

signature reservation on the recto;

serial reservations;

tactile reservations;

outer frame datum.

The mask geometry also resolves otherwise mirrorable scene relationships. Recto scenic density is biased to the left and centre inside `R-IMG`. Verso scenic density is biased to the centre and right inside `V-IMG`. When a local scene description specifies a directional path, that explicit path governs; when it only establishes opposite sides or a diagonal without naming frame direction, the denser secondary mass occupies the image-field side farther from the protected value and institutional masks.

Keep the palette in solar ochre and warm yellow-brown, modelled primarily through lightness. Scene-specific exclusions: no people, exterior launch pad, visitor complex, or heroic launch spectacle.

| €10 | low-angle self-supporting lattice mobile tower | high-angle aligned three-dish radiocommunications complex | singular node / broader network |
| €20 | ground-level tram intersection and station | high-angle container port and logistics links | circulation of people / circulation of goods |

| €200 | near-horizontal orbital upper stage with Earth | interior rocket integration hall | aspiration / assembly capability |

The relationship is conceptual and spatial.

The recto stays close to an emblematic experience.

The verso reveals a broader apparatus, a less visible system, or a different manifestation that deepens the denomination's meaning.

The reverse never functions as a zoomed-out copy of the front.

Elevated system views are intentional verso grammar. The €5, €10, €20, and €100 versos use the same broad high elevated oblique camera class so the reverse consistently reads as a reveal of organised systems; denomination distinction on those sides comes from silhouette, spatial type, directional geometry, and tonal mass rather than from camera height alone. The €50 and €200 versos remain elevated interior views with their already specified first-balcony and high-gallery positions.

## 24. Continuity across denominations

Cross-denomination continuity is carried by the dependency narrative and the common visual grammar.

The €5 power system provides the substrate for the €10 communications system.

The €10 communications system provides the coordination required by the €20 movement of people and logistics.

The €20's circulation creates access and encounters that feed the €50 processes of learning, judgment, and cultural interpretation.

The €50's interpretive institutions support the belonging expressed by the €100 garden and stewardship landscape.

The €100 preserves environmental conditions inherited by the €200 future.

The €200 integration hall visibly depends on electricity, communication, mobility, interpretation, environmental management, and accumulated institutional capability, returning the sequence to its foundations.

Cross-denomination links are carried by the sequence itself rather than by cameo objects inserted solely to signal continuity.

## 29. Evaluation summary

### 29.1 Visual harmony and coherence

All six notes share:

a horizontal monetary structure;

matte architectural visualisation;

temperate or boreal European environmental treatment for exterior scenes;

functional unornamented architecture;

selective anonymous human silhouettes;

denomination-led hue families;

stable institutional elements;

strong silhouette hierarchy;

controlled quiet space;

consistent crop discipline.

Coherence comes from common design grammar rather than identical scene construction.

### 29.2 Thematic consistency

Every denomination names a broad system and resolves it through concrete manifestations.

The recto presents an immediate experience.

For €5, €10, €100, and €200, the verso reveals a broader apparatus or hidden system that supports the recto experience. The €20 and €50 deliberately use complementary manifestations instead: circulation of goods broadens Mobility, and orchestral cultural form broadens Interpretation.

The six roles form one functional sequence from physical substrate to open future.

### 29.3 Visual appeal and aesthetic quality

The revision prioritises clear dominant forms over scene quantity.

Each side remains visually understandable before close details are inspected.

The €20 gains a strong contrast between lived urban mobility and hidden port logistics.

The €50 deliberately crosses from intellectual interpretation to cultural form without collapsing the denomination into one art form.

The €100 recto remains intimate and simple; the €100 verso remains richer, with all complexity organised by one continuous watershed.

The €200 shifts from an external space-centre tableau to a more specific interior integration system.

### 29.4 Symbolism and cultural representation of the EU

The series represents Europe through shared capability:

electricity infrastructure;

telecommunications;

urban mobility and logistics;

higher education and cultural institutions;

environmental stewardship;

future-facing engineering and exploration.

Exterior settings use plausible temperate and boreal European landscapes without binding the series to one nation.

The imagery remains anonymous enough to outlive specific operators and institutions.

### 29.5 Denomination discrimination

Independent signals include:

physical proportion;

colour family;

dominant recto silhouette;

dominant verso silhouette;

border rhythm;

tonal distribution;

tactile group;

spatial type.

### 29.6 Public recognition

The revised scenes use concrete objects and spatial systems rather than abstract institutional composites.

Transmission towers, reactor domes and cooling towers, aligned satellite dishes, tram tracks, a container ship and cranes, a university lecture hall, an unoccupied orchestral stage, a public garden, an orbital upper stage, and a rocket integration hall provide immediate anchors.

### 29.7 Accessibility

Large numerals, strong local contrast, tactile reservations, denomination-specific silhouettes, grayscale separation, and simplified scene hierarchy support accessibility.

Final accessibility performance requires specialist testing.

### 29.8 Creativity and originality

The proposal translates the optimistic worldview associated with Frutiger Aero into a restrained monetary and architectural language.

Its originality lies less in surface decoration than in using clean technological, cultural, urban, and environmental imagery to construct a dependency narrative across six denominations.

## 30. Closing statement

Living Systems begins with the physical substrate of modern life and ends with an object already in orbit.

Between those points, electricity enables communication, communication enables the coordinated circulation of people and goods, mobility broadens interpretation, interpretation produces belonging, belonging motivates stewardship, and stewardship preserves the ability to attempt what does not yet exist.

The series does not try to show everything each concept can mean.

It makes visual decisions.

A denomination can use more than one manifestation when the contrast between its two sides clarifies the broader system more effectively than repeating one object family.

Most scenes have one dominant organising subject.

The €100 alone becomes deliberately plural at landscape scale because environment is itself a network of relationships.

The result is intended to feel optimistic without becoming nostalgic, technological without becoming corporate, environmental without becoming promotional, cultural without becoming ceremonial spectacle, and futuristic without becoming science-fiction theatre.

The notes present public capability as something built, maintained, interpreted, shared, and inherited.

## Why the revision was necessary

Version 3 had largely resolved scene hierarchy, camera ambiguity, colour control, and prompt determinism, but the latest generation exposed three remaining classes of instability.

First, the proposal specified objects more precisely than their surrounding sites. The generator therefore filled unspecified ground with a dry scrubland default and placed technical structures as isolated objects in that terrain.

Second, some denominations still followed the weak pattern of showing one manifestation on the recto and a larger version of the same manifestation on the verso. The €20 rail pair was the clearest remaining case.

Third, several scenes were still conceptually narrower than their denomination. The €50 theatre pair reduced Interpretation too strongly to formal performance, while the €200 exterior space centre repeated familiar launch-site iconography instead of revealing engineering construction.

Version 4 therefore stabilises environment and site context at series level, broadens €20 and €50 through deliberately different manifestations across their two sides, and makes the €200 verso a more specific system-of-capability scene.
